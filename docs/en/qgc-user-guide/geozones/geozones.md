# Geozones (ED-269 / ED-318)

_QGroundControl_ can load the UAS geographical zones (geozones) published by aviation authorities, show them on the maps, check planned missions against them and alert you when a vehicle approaches or enters them.

::: warning
These checks are advisory. Always check the official sources and obtain any required authorisation before flying.
:::

## Overview

The following data is supported (see the [format support matrix](#format-support-matrix) for the details of each format):

- EUROCAE ED-269 (JSON) and ED-318 (GeoJSON) geozone files, feeds and services.
- AIXM 5.1 airspaces, like the UK flight restriction zones published by NATS.
- The temporary flight restrictions (TFRs) published by the FAA.
- The zones of the German DiPUL and French IGN map services.
- The FAA's public airspace layers for drones in the United States: UAS facility maps, national security UAS flight restrictions, controlled airspace and special use airspace (informational).
- The airspaces of OpenAIP, unofficial community data (CC BY-NC 4.0) for regions without an official source.

The zones are shown on the map in both the Plan and Fly views, colored by restriction: prohibited (red), authorisation required (orange), conditional (yellow), no restriction (green) and U-space airspace (blue).
Zones whose published restriction isn't recognised are shown as unknown (gray), and are checked and alerted like conditional zones.

Where the zones are used:

- **Plan View**: the [mission check](#mission-check) warns when the planned flight enters zones, suggests [routes around them](#route-around-zones), exports a [pre-flight briefing](#pre-flight-briefing) and the [operational intent](#operational-intent) for a flight authorisation request, records the data the plan was checked against ([plan data audit](#plan-data-audit)) and [adds zones to the GeoFence](#add-geozones-to-the-geofence).
- **Fly View**: [flight alerts](#flight) warn when a connected vehicle approaches or enters a zone, and guided commands are checked before they are sent.
- **Application Settings > Geozones**: the sources, authorisations and [settings](#settings-reference).

When zones are loaded, the **Geozones** button in the Plan View tool strip and the geozone indicator in the Fly View toolbar show a color legend, the number of loaded zones and sources, when the data was last pulled (like _Data pulled 09:41 today_, the latest download, server confirmation or file load of the sources turned on) and any outdated or expired data. The Plan View panel starts with the zone search and also has a **Show geozones** switch, and the expanded Fly View indicator has the display and flight alert settings. **Sources (n) ›** in the Plan View panel and **Manage Geozones...** in the Fly View indicator open the geozone settings. The **Geozones** button of the Plan View is also there before any zone is loaded, since its panel is where the zones of the map area are downloaded, your own zones are drawn and the sources are opened.

## Loading Data {#loading-data}

### Files and Feeds {#files-and-feeds}

To add geozones:

1. Open _Application Settings > Geozones_, or press **Sources (n) ›** in the Plan View **Geozones** panel or **Manage Geozones...** in the Fly View geozone indicator.
1. Press **Add File...** and select one or more geozone files (`.json`, `.geojson`, AIXM `.xml` or `.zip` packages), or press **Add Feed...**, select a national feed (or **Other URL** to enter an `https://` address) and press **Download**, or press **Add Service...** for a service needing credentials or an area (see [Geozone Services](#geozone-services)).

The national feeds offered by **Add Feed...** are **Finland (Traficom)**, **Latvia (LGS)**, **Luxembourg (Geoportail)**, **Sweden (LFV)**, **United States TFRs (FAA)**, **Switzerland (FOCA)**, **Germany aviation zones (DiPUL)**, **United Kingdom FRZ (NATS)**, **Spain aeronautical zones (ENAIRE)**, **Spain urban zones (ENAIRE)**, **Netherlands (IenW)** and **Austria (Austro Control)**. Feeds already added are grayed out as _(added)_, as is **Download** for an **Other URL** already added: turn them on or refresh them in the list of sources instead. Feeds without a title of their own are named after the feed, like _Austria (Austro Control)_.
**Germany aviation zones (DiPUL)** holds the control zones, airports, aerodromes and flight restriction areas of the German DiPUL service (licensed CC BY-ND 4.0), and **United Kingdom FRZ (NATS)** the flight restriction and runway protection zones of protected aerodromes and the danger areas from the AIXM dataset NATS publishes every AIRAC cycle.
German zone types are mapped to restrictions: restricted areas and temporary no-fly zones are prohibited, aviation, sensitive (including hospitals) and nature zones require authorisation, and residential areas, roads, railways and waterways are conditional.

The Spanish, Dutch and Austrian feeds are the official publications of the UAS geographical zones of these countries (EU 2019/947 article 15):

- **Spain aeronautical zones (ENAIRE)** and **Spain urban zones (ENAIRE)**: the ED-318 files ENAIRE publishes on its [AIP page of UAS geographical zones](https://aip.enaire.es/AIP/UAS-en.html), updated on AIRAC dates: the zones around aerodromes and heliports, the prohibited, restricted and segregated areas, the areas restricted to aerial photography (`ZGUAS_Aero`, about 1,700 zones) and the urban zones (`ZGUAS_Urbano`). All require authorisation (or coordination) between the limits of each zone; a zone around an aerodrome often starts above the height up to which no coordination is needed, like _45 m AGL – 900 m AGL_. The messages, contacts and details of the zones are those of ENAIRE (in Spanish). ENAIRE's infrastructure zones, mostly railways (about 14,000 zones), are downloaded by area, see [Zones of the Map Area](#zones-of-the-map-area).
- **Netherlands (IenW)**: the ED-269 file of the Dutch zones which the ministry of Infrastructure and Water Management links from its [drone page](https://www.luchtvaartindetoekomst.nl/onderwerpen/o/onbemande-luchtvaart) (the version of February 2026 has 743 zones). The feed reads the page and downloads the file it links, so a new version is picked up at the next refresh. The PDOK _Drone no-fly zones_ service was taken out of production in July 2026.
- **Austria (Austro Control)**: the ED-269 datasets Austro Control lists on its page of [geographical UAS zones](https://www.austrocontrol.at/luftfahrtbehoerde/lizenzen__bewilligungen/drohnen/geografische_zonen) with the date each takes effect; the dataset in effect today is downloaded, and the next one once its date has come. The full message of each zone is taken from its localized messages, in the language of _QGroundControl_ when available (German) or else English.

The data stays the publishers': ENAIRE asks to be credited as its owner, and Austro Control's site reserves the use of its contents, so check their terms before redistributing the data, for example in [offline packs](#provenance-and-offline-packs).

Several files and feeds can be loaded at the same time. Files and downloads larger than 100 MB are rejected. Zip archives are read directly; when a package holds several geozone files (`.json`, `.geojson` or AIXM `.xml`), they are loaded together as one source, zones published in more than one file are kept once, and files of another format, like metadata, are left out. When a server answers with an error document or a web page instead of zones, its message is shown. Loading the same file again (from the same folder), or a feed with the same URL, replaces its zones; files with the same name in different folders are separate sources.
Files are loaded in the background, and the Plan View map is moved to the new zones if they are not in view. When several files are selected together, one message tells how many were added and why the others failed, like _3 geozone files added, 1 failed: zones.json: Invalid JSON_. The file dialogs of Android, iOS and of Linux desktops without a native file dialog select one file at a time.

_QGroundControl_ keeps a copy of each source and reloads them automatically on startup. Feeds are checked for updates when they are older than 24 hours (15 minutes for TFRs) by default, or sooner when the server tells how long its data stays current (HTTP `Cache-Control: max-age` or `Expires`) and that is shorter, though not more often than every 5 minutes. For feeds read from a download page, like those of NATS, the Netherlands and Austria, it is the lifetime of the dataset which counts, not that of the page. **Refresh Feeds** checks them immediately. Feeds which have not changed on the server are not downloaded again and keep the date of their data, and a feed republished with only new dates or a new title keeps its zones. If a download fails, the previous data is kept and the error, such as an untrusted certificate or a server which didn't respond within 2 minutes, is shown below the source. A failed feed is tried again after 5 minutes, then twice as long after each further failure, up to 6 hours or its refresh interval; **Refresh Feeds** tries at once.

Each feed shows when it is checked next, like _next check 11/11/26 09:41_. When the controller has no network at that time, nothing is lost: the stored data stays in use and the feed is refreshed as soon as the controller is online again. _QGroundControl_ follows the network state the system reports: while it is offline, due feeds wait (_next check when online_) instead of failing, and when it comes back online, the feeds which are due or failed for lack of a network are downloaded at once (also a refresh you asked for, or the download of the area the vehicle or the mission moved to, which failed meanwhile), as are feeds which became due while _QGroundControl_ was closed, at startup. A download which failed because no server could be reached (no network, unknown host, refused or timed out connection) is retried at least every 15 minutes, also on systems which don't report the network state; failures of the server itself (like _503 Service Unavailable_) keep the longer retry delays.
If a new copy can't be saved, for example because the disk is full, the previous copy is kept and a message is shown. The previous copy is also kept while the list of stored sources naming the new one can't be saved, so the source is still there at the next start. If the list of stored sources can't be read, the stored copies are moved to a `backup-<date>` folder in the `GeoZones` folder of the application data location so they can be added again; a list saved by a newer version of _QGroundControl_ is left untouched. Other files in the `GeoZones` folder, like your own notes or exports, are never moved or removed: at startup only stored copies no source uses anymore are cleaned up.

The **United States TFRs (FAA)** feed lists the current temporary flight restrictions. The details of each NOTAM are downloaded with the list for its effective times and altitudes, and kept for offline use; only new or changed NOTAMs are downloaded again. A TFR whose details can't be downloaded is treated as prohibited at all times and altitudes. Where a NOTAM has several areas, its TFRs span the altitudes of all of them; when one of the areas doesn't give a limit, or gives it in an unknown unit or another reference than the others, the TFRs have no floor or no ceiling. Check the NOTAM for exceptions.

European NOTAMs can't be loaded, since EUROCONTROL only provides them to organisations which signed its data user agreement.

### Zones of the Map Area {#zones-of-the-map-area}

Some services serve their zones by area. In the Plan View **Geozones** panel, **Map Area ▾** opens **Download zones for the map area**, which downloads the zones of the map area, up to about 100 km wide, from **Germany, all zones (DiPUL)** (including residential areas, hospitals and nature reserves), **France, open category (IGN)** (where flights are prohibited or limited to a maximum height) or **Spain infrastructure zones (ENAIRE)** (the zones protecting railways and other infrastructure, from the feature service of ENAIRE's AIS: mostly _Conditional_ up to 120 m AGL, with the operator to notify, like ADIF 20 days before the flight). The provider covering the map is chosen. A provider whose downloaded area already covers the whole map view is grayed out as _(downloaded here)_ and **Download** is disabled; move the map or zoom out to download more. Downloaded areas are kept for offline use and refreshed like feeds. When the server limits its responses, the further pages are downloaded too (up to 50). A response which stays incomplete never passes for a complete one: when a WFS server tells that zones are still missing (a page failed, or it has more pages), the zones received are loaded, a warning below the source tells how many are missing, and the download counts as a failed update, which is retried sooner and degrades the [geo-awareness status](#geo-awareness); other services, which don't tell how many zones are missing, fail the download and keep the zones loaded before. Download a smaller area then.

In the United States, the same list offers the FAA's public ArcGIS layers (no account needed), each a source of its own:

- **United States UAS facility maps (FAA)**: the grid cells of controlled airspace around airports, as **Authorisation required** from the ground up, with the height LAANC can authorise in the conditions (_LAANC up to 200 ft AGL_). Cells at 0 ft are **Prohibited**: flights there need further coordination with the FAA (FAADroneZone).
- **United States national security UAS restrictions (FAA)**: the 14 CFR 99.7 restrictions over military and other sensitive facilities, **Prohibited** between their floor and ceiling (usually up to 400 ft AGL), with the facility's contact. The layer gives the limits as text, like _Surface_, _400' AGL_ or _0-400 FT AGL_ (a range gives its upper bound as ceiling). A text which isn't understood entirely, gives no reference (`AGL` or `MSL`) or puts the ceiling at the surface leaves that limit unknown, with a warning: the restriction then has no ceiling, or starts at the ground.
- **United States controlled airspace (FAA)**: class B, C and D airspace and the class E surface areas, **Authorisation required** between their floor and ceiling. Class A, Mode C veils and class E airspace starting above the ground are left out.
- **United States special use airspace (FAA)**: prohibited areas (**Prohibited**), restricted areas (**Authorisation required**, with their times of use as conditions), and warning, alert and military operations areas (**Conditional**).

The FAA publishes these layers for information (_not for navigation_): the zones say so in their details, and the sources are titled _(FAA, informational)_. Check B4UFLY or a LAANC provider before flying, and the **United States TFRs (FAA)** feed for temporary restrictions. The FAA services limit how many requests a client sends per minute; a download refused for it reports _Too many requests_ and can be repeated a minute later. The layers are also offered by **Add Service...**, following the mission, the vehicle and the map view (facility maps up to 10,000 km²).

### Geozone Services {#geozone-services}

**Add Service...** adds a feed from a service which needs credentials, an ED-318 query or the area of interest. Pick a service, or **Other service**, check its address and settings, enter its secret if it needs one and press **Download**:

- **ED-318 geozone service**: a service following the retrieval interface of EUROCAE ED-318, which is sent the area and time of interest in a POST request (`geometry`, `startDateTime`, `endDateTime`) and answers with the zones in `uasZones`. Enter the address of its UAS zones endpoint.
- **Germany ED-318 service (DFS)**: the ED-318 interface of the DFS Geospatial Information Service (`https://dipul-service.dfs.de/api/geofencing/v2/uas-zones`), with the user and password of a dipul account, which DFS grants on request through the dipul contact form. It answers areas up to 100 km², so the margin around the mission and the vehicle is reduced to fit, and the zones of a larger mission area are not requested.
- **Zones of the area (GET)**: a service taking the area in its address. Write `{bbox}` (west,south,east,north), `{west}`, `{south}`, `{east}`, `{north}`, `{lat}`, `{lon}` (the center), `{radiusM}`, `{radiusKm}`, `{radiusNm}`, `{start}` or `{end}` (ISO 8601 UTC) where the service expects them, for example `https://example.org/zones?bbox={bbox}`.
- **Airspaces (OpenAIP, unofficial)**: the airspaces of [OpenAIP](https://www.openaip.net) in the area of interest (`https://api.core.openaip.net/api/airspaces?bbox={bbox}`, up to 40,000 km², all pages), with the API key of an API client created in your openaip.net profile (sent in the `x-openaip-api-key` header). OpenAIP is unofficial community data under CC BY-NC 4.0 (attribution, non-commercial use only), not a geozone publisher, so it is never added by default: the source, the zones' details and the briefing data versions say _OpenAIP (unofficial, CC BY-NC 4.0)_. Prohibited areas are **Prohibited**; restricted areas, control zones, aerodrome traffic zones and class A–D control areas **Authorisation required**; danger, training, gliding, TMZ, RMZ and protected areas **Conditional**, with their activation (on demand, by NOTAM) as conditions and their hours of operation and primary frequency in the details. Flight information regions, airways, sectors and class E–G control areas are left out. Limits in meters are converted to feet.
- **Airspaces of a country (OpenAIP, unofficial)**: OpenAIP's daily GeoJSON export of a country's airspaces, without an account: replace `ch` in `https://storage.openaip.net/openaip-system-exports/ch_asp.geojson` with the two-letter code of the country. The same terms and mapping apply.
- **United States advisories (Airspace Link)**: configuration only. The Airspace Link AirHub API briefs an area posted as GeoJSON (`/v2/briefing/b4ufly/area`), with OAuth2 client credentials and an `x-api-key` header, which Airspace Link grants on request; _QGroundControl_ does not read its notices yet.
- **United States UAS facility maps / national security UAS restrictions / controlled airspace / special use airspace (FAA)**: the FAA layers of [Zones of the Map Area](#zones-of-the-map-area), following the mission, the vehicle and the map view.

The authentication can be an API key in a header or a query parameter, a bearer token, a user and password (HTTP basic authentication) or OAuth2 client credentials, for which _QGroundControl_ requests a token from the token URL with the client id and secret and requests a new one when it expires or is refused. **Zones member** names the member of a JSON response holding the zones, like `data.geojson`, when the service wraps them; ED-318 replies are unwrapped automatically. Services, like other feeds, are refreshed periodically and kept for offline use.

Secrets (API keys, tokens, passwords and client secrets) are kept unencrypted, since _QGroundControl_ has no secure store which works on all its platforms, in a file of their own which only your user account can read (`GeoZoneCredentials.json` in the application data location, next to the `GeoZones` folder, not in it); the **Add Service...** form says so. Secrets which an earlier version kept in the _QGroundControl_ settings are moved to that file at the next start. They are never written to the settings, the list of sources, the logs, the provenance or exported packs, and are only sent to the server of the service, not after a redirect to another server. Custom headers of a service, which may hold keys too, are only sent to that server as well, and are left out of the provenance and of exported packs. Removing a source removes its secret, and so does changing the authentication or the token URL of a service, since the secret was entered for them. A source whose secret is missing, like after importing a pack, says so and has a field to enter it.

The token URL and the SHA-256 URL of a service are normally on the server of the service. When one of them is on another server, which would receive the client secret or the area of interest, **Add Service...** names that server and only adds the service once **I trust these servers** is checked. The check is for the servers named when it was made: it is cleared when the addresses or the template of the form change so that other servers are named, and a service which would send requests to a server which wasn't confirmed is refused.

### Area of Interest {#area-of-interest}

With **Download the zones around the mission, the vehicle and the map view**, a service is requested for the area of interest: the bounds of the planned mission, the surroundings of the active vehicle and the area shown on the Fly or Plan map, with a margin of 5 km around them. The source shows the area downloaded. When the mission, the surroundings of the vehicle or the map view leave that area, the zones of the new area are downloaded, unless **Download services around the mission and vehicle** is turned off in the settings; refreshes keep the area while it still covers the area of interest. A service with an area is only added once a mission is planned, a vehicle is connected or a map was shown.

The surroundings of the vehicle look ahead, so that the zones beyond the area downloaded are known before the vehicle reaches them: they are its position and the position it reaches on its course in 90 seconds, with the distance it flies in 90 seconds around them, at least 2 km. The next area is therefore requested while the vehicle is still that far from the edge of the area downloaded (2 km for a vehicle slower than 22 m/s, 4.5 km at 50 m/s), which leaves time for the download and for the alert of a zone beyond the edge. A download which fails while the vehicle flies on is tried again within a minute.

A service which answers areas up to a size is never asked for more. The margin and the look-ahead shrink together to fit, so a small limit means more frequent downloads and less look-ahead for a fast vehicle. If the mission and the vehicle together still don't fit, the zones around the vehicle are downloaded (around the center of the mission without a vehicle) and the source reports that the mission is larger than the area the service answers, which counts as a failed update for the [geo-awareness status](#geo-awareness): zones of the rest of the mission are unknown.

The map view is taken a second after the map stops moving, so panning and zooming don't send a request per frame, and nothing is downloaded while the view stays inside the area downloaded. Views wider or higher than 200 km count with their central 200 km, so zooming out doesn't request a whole continent. The view only joins the mission and the vehicle when all of them fit in 200 km (and in the area the service answers, like the 100 km² of the DFS service): looking at the map far from a planned mission or a flying vehicle never replaces the zones around them. Without a mission or a vehicle, the view alone is downloaded, cut around its center for services answering smaller areas. Turn off **Download services for the map view** to only follow the mission and the vehicle.

### Adding or Changing Feeds {#adding-or-changing-feeds}

Feeds change over time: publishers move their files, new countries publish their zones, and a manufacturer may want to offer its own list. Feeds can be added or changed without a new version of _QGroundControl_ in three ways.

**A single feed** – **Add Feed...** > **Other URL** downloads any `https://` (or `http://`, `file://`) address of a geozone file: an ED-269 or ED-318 file, a zip archive of them, AIXM 5.1, or the GeoJSON of the formats in the [format support matrix](#format-support-matrix). It is refreshed like the built-in feeds. A service which needs credentials or takes the area, like a company ED-318 server or a WFS with a `{bbox}` parameter, is added with **Add Service...** (see [Geozone Services](#geozone-services)).

**A feed catalogue** – the list of feeds **Add Feed...**, **Download zones for the map area** and **Add Service...** offer is a catalogue in JSON, which can be extended or changed:

- with a file named `feeds.json` in the `GeoZones` folder of the application data location (on Linux `~/.local/share/QGroundControl/QGroundControl/GeoZones`, on Windows `%APPDATA%\QGroundControl\QGroundControl\GeoZones`). The settings show the folder below **Feed catalogue URL**. The file is read at startup and again when it changes (checked every 5 minutes and by **Refresh Feeds**).
- with the **Feed catalogue URL** setting (_Application Settings > Geozones > Data Updates_): the `https://` address of a catalogue, like one the manufacturer publishes. A catalogue changes the feeds offered, so it isn't downloaded over `http://`, where it could be changed on its way, except from a server on the controller itself (`localhost`); a warning says so. It is downloaded at startup, after the feed refresh interval (24 hours by default) and by **Refresh Feeds**, and its last valid copy is kept for starts without a network. On Android controllers like the Signav Pro, whose application data folder isn't accessible, use the URL; a `file://` address of a file on the controller works too.

Entries of these catalogues with the `id` of a built-in entry replace it, other entries are added, and `"enabled": false` removes the built-in entry with that `id`. The URL's catalogue applies first, then `feeds.json`. Below **Add Feed...**, _Feed catalogue: built-in feeds, 2 change(s) from feeds.json_ tells which catalogues are used. A catalogue which can't be read (invalid JSON, a newer version) is ignored, and an entry which can't be used (no name, an unknown format, a URL without `{bbox}` for an area service) is left out; a warning below the sources says which and why. So does a member which isn't read as written: an entry with a member of the wrong type, like `"enabled": "false"` in quotes, or a service with an unknown `method` or `auth`, is left out rather than guessed, and unknown members, like misspelled ones, are named and ignored.

What a catalogue can and cannot change:

- It can add feeds, area services and services to the lists, and replace a built-in entry (its address, name, format or notes) by one with the same `id`.
- It can turn built-in entries off with `"enabled": false`, so that they are no longer offered. A catalogue which can't be read never does: the built-in feeds are then all kept. When the catalogue of the **Feed catalogue URL** turns built-in entries off, the warning below the sources names them, like _The feed catalogue of https://… turns off 2 built-in feed(s): Latvia (LGS), United States TFRs (FAA)_, since a downloaded catalogue then takes official feeds away.
- It cannot change the sources already added: they stay in the list of sources with the address they were added with, are refreshed from it and keep their zones, whatever the catalogue says later. A catalogue changes what is offered, not the sources loaded: to move a source to a new address, remove it and add the feed again.
- It cannot change settings or turn checks and alerts off, and the secret of a service is never read from it: the user enters it.

A complete example, which adds a feed, a service serving its zones by area, a feed whose current file is linked from a download page, a company ED-318 service, replaces the URL of the Swiss feed and removes the Latvian one:

```json
{
    "fileType": "GeoZoneFeeds",
    "version": 1,
    "feeds": [
        {
            "id": "acme-nl",
            "country": "NL",
            "name": "Netherlands (Acme mirror)",
            "url": "https://zones.acme.example/nl/UASZoneVersion.json",
            "format": "ed269",
            "refreshHours": 12,
            "licence": "CC0 1.0",
            "attribution": "Ministerie van Infrastructuur en Waterstaat",
            "info": "https://zones.acme.example/nl/",
            "note": "Checked and mirrored by Acme every day."
        },
        {
            "id": "acme-area",
            "country": "DE",
            "name": "Germany, Acme zones by area",
            "areaUrl": "https://zones.acme.example/wfs?service=WFS&version=2.0.0&request=GetFeature&typeNames=zones&outputFormat=application/json&bbox={bbox},EPSG:4326",
            "coverage": [5.8, 47.2, 15.1, 55.2],
            "format": "ed318",
            "serviceTemplate": { "maxAreaKm2": 10000, "note": "Follows the mission, the vehicle and the map view." }
        },
        {
            "id": "acme-airac",
            "name": "Acme AIRAC datasets",
            "url": "https://zones.acme.example/datasets/",
            "format": "ed269",
            "datasetLink": { "pattern": "href=\"([^\"]{0,300}_(\\d{8})\\.zip)\"", "dateFormat": "yyyyMMdd" },
            "airac": true
        },
        {
            "id": "foca",
            "country": "CH",
            "name": "Switzerland (FOCA)",
            "url": "https://data.geo.admin.ch/ch.bazl.einschraenkungen-drohnen/einschraenkungen-drohnen/einschraenkungen-drohnen_4326.json"
        },
        { "id": "lgs", "enabled": false }
    ],
    "services": [
        {
            "id": "acme-ed318",
            "name": "Acme ED-318 service",
            "url": "https://utm.acme.example/api/uas-zones",
            "note": "Use the account Acme gave you.",
            "service": { "method": "ed318Post", "auth": "basic", "areaOfInterest": true, "maxAreaKm2": 400 }
        }
    ]
}
```

The members of a catalogue:

| Member | Description |
| ------ | ----------- |
| `fileType` | `GeoZoneFeeds` (optional). |
| `version` | `1`. Catalogues of a later version are ignored. |
| `feeds` | The feeds and the services serving zones by area. |
| `services` | The services offered by **Add Service...**. |

The members of a feed:

| Member | Required | Description |
| ------ | -------- | ----------- |
| `id` | Yes | Identifies the entry, to replace or remove it, like `acme-nl`. |
| `name` | Yes | Shown in the lists, and as the name of the sources added from the entry: the title the data gives itself is shown in their details. |
| `country` | | ISO 3166-1 two-letter code of the country of the data, like `NL`. |
| `url` | `url` or `areaUrl` | Address of the whole dataset, offered by **Add Feed...**: the file, a zip archive, or a download page with `datasetLink`. |
| `areaUrl` | `url` or `areaUrl` | Address of the zones of an area, with the placeholders of **Zones of the area (GET)**: `{bbox}` (west,south,east,north in degrees), `{west}`, `{south}`, `{east}`, `{north}`, `{lat}`, `{lon}`, `{radiusM}`, `{radiusKm}`, `{radiusNm}`, `{start}`, `{end}`. Offered by **Download zones for the map area**, for areas up to about 100 km. Further pages of WFS (`numberMatched`), ArcGIS (`exceededTransferLimit` with `resultRecordCount` and `resultOffset`) and numbered (`page`, `totalPages`) responses are downloaded too. |
| `coverage` | With `areaUrl` | `[west, south, east, north]` in degrees: where the service has zones. The whole world when left out. |
| `format` | | `ed269`, `ed318`, `aixm`, `dipul`, `france-uas`, `faa-tfr`, `faa-airspace` or `openaip`. Left out, the format is recognised from the content. Give it when responses can be empty, like those of a WFS for an area without zones. |
| `match` | | `{ "host": ..., "path": ... }`: the addresses of the service, when they differ from the host of `url` or `areaUrl`, like several services on one server told apart by their path. Addresses of the service get its format. |
| `datasetLink` | | For a `url` which is a download page: `pattern`, a regular expression whose first capture is the link to a dataset and whose second capture, if any, is the date it takes effect in `dateFormat` (`yyyyMMdd` by default, like `d.M.yyyy` for 25.10.2026). The latest dataset in effect is downloaded, or the first link found without a date. Write `\\` for a backslash in JSON. The pattern only searches the page at the `url` of its own feed (pages up to 1 MB), never the pages of other feeds, and the link is only followed to an `https://` address (to an `http://` one only from a page which was itself read over `http://`), never to a file. | `live` | | `true` for short-lived restrictions: refreshed with **Live restrictions refresh interval** and outdated after 2 hours. |
| `licence`, `attribution`, `info`, `note` | | Shown below the list when the feed is chosen: the licence of the data, its owner, the page of the publisher about it, and a note. |
| `serviceTemplate` | | With `areaUrl`: also offer the service in **Add Service...**, following the mission, the vehicle and the map view. `{ "maxAreaKm2": ..., "note": ... }` or `true`. |
| `enabled` | | `false` removes the entry with this `id` from the catalogues applied before (the built-in one). |

The members of a service: `id`, `name`, `url` (the address or URL template, empty for the user to enter), `note`, `format`, `supported` (`false` for services _QGroundControl_ can't read yet, offered for configuration only), `nameFromData` (`true` when its sources take the title of their data), `enabled`, and `service` with the settings of the **Add Service...** form: `method` (`get` or `ed318Post`), `auth` (`none`, `apiKeyHeader`, `apiKeyQuery`, `bearer`, `basic` or `oauth2ClientCredentials`), `authParameter` (the header or query parameter of an API key), `user`, `tokenUrl` and `scope` (OAuth2), `headers` (an object of further request headers), `responseMember` (the member holding the zones, like `data.geojson`), `areaOfInterest`, `timeWindowHours`, `maxAreaKm2` and `sha256Url`. Secrets never go into a catalogue: the user enters them in the form.

To test a feed before publishing it, add its address with **Add Feed...** > **Other URL**: the source shows its format, zone count and the problems found, and its zones can be checked on the map and in their details. Developers can check files and downloads with the `geozone-validate` tool (see the [developer guide](../../qgc-dev-guide/geozones.md#validating-files)). A catalogue is tested by placing it as `feeds.json` and checking the warning below the sources and the list of **Add Feed...**.

### Integrity Checks {#integrity-checks}

If a server sends the SHA-256 of its data in a `Repr-Digest`, `Content-Digest` or `Digest` header, or a service publishes it in a file whose address is given in **SHA-256 URL** (like `{url}.sha256`, in the format `sha256sum` writes), the data is checked against it (the headers of compressed responses are not checked, since they give the SHA-256 of the compressed data). Verified sources say so. Data which doesn't match, or whose SHA-256 can't be downloaded, isn't loaded: the previous data is kept and the source shows that the integrity check failed. Feeds are checked by SHA-256 only; offline packs can be signed, see [Signed Packs](#signed-packs).

### Managing Sources {#managing-sources}

Each source is listed by the name of its feed in the list of feeds, like _Netherlands (IenW)_, or the name of its service; other feeds and files by the title of their data, or their server or file name without one. Each source shows its format, the title of its data when it isn't its name, its zone count and date: the issue date of the data or the date of a file, the local date and time feeds were downloaded, and the local date and time files were loaded. For feeds it also shows when the server last confirmed the data current (**checked**, date and time) and when the feed is checked next (**next check**, or _when online_), and for datasets published by AIRAC cycle (like the NATS dataset) the cycle of the data. After a reload (a refresh, a download or an imported pack) which changed zones, the source shows when and how many zones were added, changed and removed, like _Updated 12:03: 3 new, 1 changed, 2 removed_; **Details** lists their names. Zones are told apart by their identifier (or their name without one) and compared by the hash of their published data. The last changes of each source are kept until the next update which changes zones, also after a restart, and are part of the provenance (`lastChanges`). A vehicle near or in a zone whose data changed is alerted again, since its limits or schedule may differ. A warning is shown if the data has expired (ED-318 `validTo`), is not valid yet, belongs to an AIRAC cycle which has ended (the next cycle took effect), or is more than 7 days old by default (2 hours for TFRs). The age of a feed counts from when its server last sent the data or confirmed it current (**checked**), whatever the issue date of the data, which is shown as information: a dataset issued months ago is current while its server still publishes it. The age of a file counts from its ED-318 or FAA issue time, or else its file date. Outdated sources are also reported by an orange **!** badge (red when the data is treated as invalid, see [Geo-Awareness Status](#geo-awareness)) on the geozone indicator in the Fly View toolbar, whose page lists them under **Outdated Data** (rather than a message when _QGroundControl_ starts), and in the Plan View warning, since they can miss zones; uploading a mission then asks for confirmation.

With **Treat outdated data as invalid** (on by default), outdated data is expired rather than possibly outdated: the source is marked **Expired** in red in the list of sources, the Plan View banner starts with a red _expired data_, the Fly View geozone indicator shows a red **!** and its page starts with _Expired geozone data_, and uploading a mission asks for confirmation (see [Mission Check](#mission-check)). Refresh or replace the source to clear it. The zones of expired data are still shown, checked and alerted: they are the best known restrictions, and hiding a restriction because its data is old would be less safe than flying with it. Turn the setting off to only warn about outdated data.

The **⋯** menu of a source has **Refresh** (feeds only), **Details**, which shows its provenance, downloaded area and the names of the zones changed by its last update, and **Remove...**. Removing a source, and **Remove All...** for all of them, ask for confirmation first since their stored copies are deleted.

Sources can publish the same zones, like a national file and a regional extract of it. A zone published alike by several sources turned on (the same identifier and country, restriction, shapes, altitude limits and schedule) is kept once, from the first of them in the list: it is drawn, checked, alerted, searched and imported into the GeoFence once, and its details and search result show how many sources publish it, like _· 2 sources_, and its details name each of them with the date of its data, as of its last check. Zones with the same identifier whose content differs are all kept, so that each is checked: where one source publishes several zones with the same identifier, name and restriction, like the areas of one restriction, each is alerted, acknowledged and imported into the GeoFence on its own. The zone count says how many zones were counted once. When most zones (over 80 %) of a file or feed you add are already loaded from another source, a note says so below the sources and in the geozone summary, until you dismiss it.
The check box of a source turns it off or on, in the settings and, with several sources, in the Fly View indicator, whose **Sources** list scrolls when there are many sources and shows long names in full when the pointer rests on them. The zones of a source turned off are not shown, checked or alerted, its outdated data isn't reported, and it stays off after a restart until it is turned on or added again.

Problems found while reading a source are shown below it and in the details of the zones concerned: altitude limits in an unknown unit are ignored, so the zone applies at all altitudes, unknown days in a schedule are taken as every day, and times of day without UTC offset are taken as UTC when the time zone of the zone's country isn't known. Zone shapes are checked too: repeated points are dropped and boundaries need not be closed, but polygons with fewer than 3 distinct points or without area, polygons touching or enclosing a pole (within about 11 km of it) and circles without a positive radius are ignored, with a warning naming them. So are shapes which can't be read: a polygon with a position which isn't a pair of numbers on the globe or with more than 200,000 points, a circle without a valid center, a geometry which isn't an area (like a line), and the shapes of a zone beyond its first 10,000. A zone which loses one of several shapes that way keeps the others, so it is smaller than published: the warning below its source and in its details tells. A zone left without any shape, or published without geometry, is counted as skipped, with a warning telling why. Airspaces a format leaves out on purpose, like the en-route airspace of OpenAIP or the Mode C veils of the FAA, aren't counted. When the answer of a feed or service has zones but none of them can be read, the download fails with an error and the source keeps the zones of its last download, rather than loading as an area without zones. An invalid hole, or one which can't be read, is ignored with a warning, so the zone covers it. A polygon whose boundary crosses itself is kept with a warning; the zone conservatively covers all the parts it encloses, also those it encloses twice (like the center of a star drawn in one stroke). Points on a zone boundary are inside the zone.

### Provenance and Offline Packs {#provenance-and-offline-packs}

**Details** of each source show where and when its data was obtained: the server which answered after redirects, its ETag or modification date, the beginning of the SHA-256 of the data received and whether it was verified or imported from a pack. The provenance is kept with each source and written to the manifest of exported packs and to the data versions of the [briefing](#pre-flight-briefing) JSON file. It holds the provider, the address requested and the one which answered with the redirects, the validators, the time of the download, the SHA-256 of the data received, the BLAKE2b-256 hash and size of the stored copy, the area downloaded and the integrity check. The manifest also gives per source its name, format, zone count, dates, service (without secrets), the zones added, changed and removed by its last update (`lastChanges`: when, the counts and up to 200 names of each) and, for AIRAC datasets, the cycle (`airac`: its name, effective date and end).

To use the zones on a computer without network access, press **Export Pack...** to save the sources turned on in a zip file, then **Import Pack...** on the other computer, which can import several packs at once. A pack holds the stored copy of each source in `sources/` and `manifest.json` with the provenance of the sources, their dates, validators, areas and services, without secrets. A key typed into the address of a feed is left out too: in the pack, the values of the address parameters named like keys, tokens, passwords or signatures (like `apikey`, `access_token` or `subscription-key`), and a password before the host, read `***`. Imported where a feed with that address and its key exists, the pack updates that feed; elsewhere the source has the address without the key, so its zones are there but it can't be refreshed until the feed is added with a key. Imported sources replace the sources with the same address or file, keep the dates and provenance of their data, marked as imported from the pack, and are refreshed like other sources when the network is available. Data in the pack which doesn't match the hashes the pack gives for it is left out, and so is a source the pack gives no hash for, also in a signed pack. That the data of a source was _verified_ against the SHA-256 of its publisher is only taken over from a pack [signed](#signed-packs) by a publisher you trust: from any other pack it is just what the pack says, so the source is shown as not verified, and it loses the mark again when you stop trusting the publisher. A pack can't change how a source already loaded is requested: such sources keep their service and secret and only take the data of the pack. New sources take the service of the pack without its custom headers, and their secret has to be entered; sources whose service would send requests to another server than their feed are left out.

### Signed Packs {#signed-packs}

Packs can be signed, so that those importing them know who made them and that they weren't changed. Press **Pack Signatures...** below the pack buttons:

- To sign the packs you export, select an Ed25519 private key file (unencrypted PEM) with **Browse...**, or create one with **New Key...**, and enter your name as **Publisher**. **New Key...** writes a file only your user account can read, and doesn't write the key where that can't be set; saving it over the key selected replaces that key, and the public key shown changes with it. Keep the key file private. The public key shown below the text lets others trust your packs: give it to them by a channel they trust. Keys can also be made with OpenSSL: `openssl genpkey -algorithm ed25519 -out geozones.pem`, whose public key `openssl pkey -in geozones.pem -pubout` prints.
- To trust the packs of a publisher, enter a name for them and their public key (base64, or PEM as OpenSSL prints it) and press **Trust**. Their keys are shown by a short id, like `681b 5d7f 40bf 39d2`, to compare with the publisher's. If the trusted publishers kept in the settings can't be read, for example after the settings file was edited or damaged, those which can't be read aren't trusted and a warning says so. The setting is then left as it is, and **Trust**, **Remove** and **Trust Publisher** change nothing, until **Discard Unreadable** keeps only the publishers which could be read.

When a pack is imported, the message and the provenance of its sources tell how it is signed: _signed by_ the name you gave a trusted publisher, _untrusted signature_ when it is signed by a key you don't trust (the source then has **Trust Publisher**, which trusts that key with the name the pack gives), or _unsigned_. A pack whose signature doesn't match its content, because it was changed after it was signed, isn't imported, nor are copies which don't match the SHA-256 the signed manifest gives. Unsigned and untrusted packs are imported too: check where they come from. Builds of _QGroundControl_ without OpenSSL (by default all but desktop Linux) can't sign packs or check signatures, and say so under **Pack Signatures...**: they export unsigned packs and import signed packs as unsigned (_signature not checked_), never as trusted.

How a zone boundary runs between two published points depends on the format. For long edges, especially at high latitudes, a great circle (geodesic) and a line straight in latitude and longitude can be several hundred meters apart:

- **ED-269, ED-318, DiPUL and IGN GeoJSON**: straight in latitude and longitude, as GeoJSON defines its lines (RFC 7946, section 3.1.1). ED-269 polygons are GeoJSON polygons and ED-318 uses the GeoJSON geometries; neither standard defines its edges otherwise.
- **AIXM 5.1**: `GeodesicString` segments are great circles. `LineStringSegment` segments in EPSG:4326 are straight in latitude and longitude, which the AIXM coding guidelines use for boundaries along parallels. Arcs (`ArcByCenterPoint`) are circles of the published radius around their center, with their angles taken as bearings. Positions are read in the coordinate reference system their element or an element enclosing it names (`srsName`): WGS 84 by latitude and longitude (EPSG:4326, also when none is named, or EPSG:4979) or by longitude and latitude (OGC CRS84 or CRS84h), with 2 values per position or 3 with a height (`srsDimension`), which isn't used. A boundary which can't be read as published isn't given another shape: its airspace volume is ignored, with a warning, when it is in another reference system, an arc has no valid center, radius (up to 1000 km) or angles, a radius or an angle has no unit or an unknown one (angles are read in degrees), a position isn't a pair of numbers on the globe, a circle is combined with other segments, or a curve runs backwards. A part of a boundary which is defined by another feature, like the borders and coasts some UK danger areas follow, or is a segment of another kind, isn't read: the parts around it are joined by a straight line, and a warning tells that the zone is approximate there. An airspace left without volume is counted as skipped. An altitude limit in another unit than ft, m or flight levels is ignored with a warning, so the airspace has no limit on that side.
- **FAA TFRs**: the NOTAM details code the boundary points of each TFR as great circle points (`GRC`), so its edges are great circles when the details are available, and straight otherwise. Circular TFRs are published as 36-sided polygons, whose sides cut slightly inside the circle (by about 7 m for a radius of 1 NM); when the NOTAM details give the circle (its center and radius), the zone is that circle instead. Polygons approximating circles in other sources are used as published.

Edges are checked as straight lines in latitude and longitude, so when zones are loaded, great circle edges get extra points on their great circle (up to 1023 per edge) and arcs a point every few degrees, keeping the boundary used by the checks, maps and fences within 1 m of the published one. On maps, long straight edges get extra points too, since the map projection would otherwise move them.

Vehicles fly the legs of a path along great circles, so the checks follow them too: legs whose great circle is more than 1 m from the line straight in latitude and longitude, like a 56 km leg along the 60th parallel which passes about 105 m north of it halfway, are checked in pieces along the great circle. Along paths whose terrain the checks look up, like guided commands, returns and alerts, the terrain is taken every 100 m (250 m for returns), for up to 1000 positions per path; on longer paths the positions are spread farther apart, which is reported as approximate (see [Approximate Results](#approximate-results)).

Altitude limits relative to the WGS84 ellipsoid are converted using the EGM96 geoid, within a few meters.

Limits published as flight levels (pressure altitude, `STD`) are approximate: their height depends on the air pressure and temperature. They are converted to heights above mean sea level with the **QNH** setting, as an altimeter set to it would read them in the ICAO standard atmosphere: the pressure altitude less 44330.8 m × (1 − (QNH / 1013.25 hPa)^0.190263), about 8.3 m lower per hPa below 1013.25 hPa. The zone details show both, like "FL 95 ≈ 2810 m AMSL (QNH 1003 hPa)". Since flight levels are lower in air colder than standard (by about 4 % of their height per 10 °C), the checks, alerts, altitude profile and strip lower floors and raise ceilings given as flight levels by 4 % of their height. Set the local QNH before planning or flying near such zones; a change applies at once to the checks and alerts.

## Map Display {#map-display}

Each restriction has a color and a letter, so that the color isn't the only cue: **P** prohibited (red, also hatched with diagonal lines), **A** authorisation required (orange), **C** conditional (yellow), **U** U-space (blue), **✓** no restriction (green) and **?** unknown (gray). The legend and the zone labels on the map show the letter. Zones that are not active at the current time are drawn with a fainter fill and hatching. Schedules based on sunrise, sunset or civil twilight are computed for the location of each zone. A daily period lasts through its end time: one published as ending at 23:59, 23:59:59 or 24:00 applies until midnight, so a zone active every day from 00:00 to 24:00 is never inactive, and one whose start and end times are the same (00:00 to 00:00) applies for the whole day. Daily times published in UTC (`08:00Z`) or with an offset from UTC (`08:00+01:00`) keep it all year. Since the formats don't tell whether times without offset (`08:00`) are UTC or local times, to be safe such a zone counts as active whenever it is in either: in the local time of the zone's country, which follows its daylight saving time, or in UTC. So a zone active from 08:00 to 18:00 in Zurich counts as active from 07:00 to 18:00 UTC in winter and from 06:00 to 18:00 UTC in summer, for the map, the flight alerts, the mission check, the routes and the briefing. The local time of European countries with one time zone on their mainland is known, with those of the Canary Islands, Madeira and the Azores; times of zones in other countries or without country are taken as UTC only, with a warning. The details of the zone show such times in local time with the assumed time zone, like "Every day 08:00-18:00 Europe/Zurich (time zone assumed)".

**Show geozones** in the Plan View **Geozones** panel (or **Show geozones on maps** in the expanded Fly View indicator and the settings) hides or shows the zones.

### Map Filters {#map-filters}

Below the legend of the Plan View **Geozones** panel and the Fly View geozone indicator, filters declutter the maps of areas with many zones:

- The check boxes of the legend hide or show the zones of each restriction, for example to see the map under large U-space airspace.
- **Only zones near the flight altitudes** hides zones whose floor is more than 100 m above, or whose ceiling is more than 100 m below, the altitude of the active vehicle in the Fly View, or the range of altitudes of the mission in the Plan View. Limits above ground are compared with the height above the terrain (of the vehicle, or along the mission), the others with the altitude above mean sea level. Without a vehicle altitude or mission, and for limits above ground where the terrain isn't known, no zones are hidden by altitude.
- **Schedule** shows **All zones**, only those **Active now**, those **Active within 12 h**: active now or within the next 12 hours, or those **Active at flight time**: active from the planned start to the estimated end of the mission (see [Flight Time](#flight-time)), widened by 15 minutes like the mission check, or active now without a planned start.
- **Source**, with several sources, shows the zones of one source only. Unlike the source check boxes, it only changes the maps.

The filters are kept between sessions and apply to both views. They only change the maps: zones hidden by them are not picked by map clicks, but the mission check, flight alerts and guided command checks still apply to them. The zones the mission check reports in the Plan View (those the mission or its returns enter) and the zones near the vehicle or reached at its velocity in the Fly View are always shown. When filters hide zones in view, a hint at the bottom of the map shows how many, with **Reset Filters**, which shows all restrictions, altitudes, schedules and sources again (also in the panels).

Click a zone on the map to see its details (restriction, altitude limits in your units, applicability schedule, reason, message, responsible authority, exemptions, U-space class, other published properties and the source it was loaded from). Where zones overlap, the details start with the number of zones at the point and a chip for each, in the color of its restriction: tap a chip to show that zone. In the Plan View this works when the click doesn't add a waypoint or rally point or select a fence. In the Fly View, when guided actions are available, the map click menu has **Geozones here**. From zoom level 12, zones centered in view also show a label with their restriction letter and name, which opens their details. Labels don't overlap each other or the mission items: where they would, the most restrictive zones are labelled first (prohibited, authorisation required, conditional, U-space, unknown), then the largest, and the others are left out until you zoom in. Zones without restriction are only labelled when the mission check or the flight alerts report them.

To find a zone, type part of its name or identifier in the search field of the Plan View **Geozones** panel or the Fly View geozone indicator, then pick a result to move the map to the zone and show its details. Case and accents don't matter (_Zurich_ finds _Zürich_), and a zone loaded from several sources is listed once.
When many small zones are in view, the smallest are left out until you zoom in, and the Plan View **Geozones** panel shows how many are hidden.

## Planning {#planning}

### Mission Check {#mission-check}

When the planned mission path or the area of a survey, corridor scan or structure scan enters a prohibited, authorisation required or conditional zone, the banner at the top of the Plan View map warns and the zones are outlined in white. The banner keeps to one line of counts, most severe first, like "● 2 prohibited · ● 3 authorisation required · returns enter 2 zones · data changed", with the actions below it (**Route Around Zones...**, **Add Exclusion Fences**, **Export ▾** for the [briefing](#pre-flight-briefing) and the [operational intent](#operational-intent)). **Details ▾** lists the zones by category (prohibited, authorisation required, conditional, unknown restriction, not excluded by the GeoFence, the zones the returns enter with one line per zone and the returns and items concerned, U-space, authorised, without restriction, not active at the flight time), why the check is approximate, outdated or changed data, the authorisations of an opened plan and the flight time checked. Click the banner to show the restricted zones on the map. With a mission and loaded zones, the banner stays visible with "No geozones on the mission path" when the mission enters none.
Besides the drawn flight path, the check follows loiter circles with a radius, the loiter of landing patterns, the distance VTOL transitions fly on past their waypoint (**VTOL TransitionDistance** in _Application Settings > Plan View_), the loops of **DO_JUMP** items, and the return the vehicle flies after a **Return To Launch** item (see [Return and Contingency Paths](#return-and-contingency-paths)). The warning names what enters zones the drawn flight path doesn't, like "Only the loiter at item 3 enters ..." or "Only the jump at item 8 enters ...".
A **DO_JUMP** flies the items from its target to the jump again as many times as its repeat count, including the straight leg back to the target, so zone schedules are checked at the times the repeats are flown. A jump repeating without end is flown once more and named "repeated without end"; very long repeats are cut off after about 5000 legs. A jump past a **Return To Launch** skips it: the check follows the items after it, whose legs the Plan View doesn't draw, as part of the jump, and only the return the vehicle reaches is flown. The mission ends at the first **Return To Launch** the vehicle reaches, with or without jumps: the flight lines of a survey or another pattern placed after it aren't checked as flown and don't count in the flight time, though the area of the pattern still is checked.
U-space airspaces the mission enters are listed too, since flying there requires a flight authorisation from a U-space service provider, and a warning is added while the Remote ID isn't ready: the operator ID isn't set (or isn't valid in the EU) in _Application Settings > Remote ID_, or the vehicle reports no Remote ID or one which isn't ready to arm. Zones without restriction are listed for their conditions, they never hide restricted zones.
Uploading a mission asks for confirmation, listing the reasons, when it enters prohibited zones you have no authorisation for (naming those the GeoFence doesn't exclude), when the geozone data of a source turned on is expired (see [Managing Sources](#managing-sources)) or when the geozone data changed since the plan was saved and you didn't acknowledge the changes (see [Plan Data Audit](#plan-data-audit)). **Cancel** leaves the mission on the vehicle as it is.

The check compares the planned altitudes with the zone altitude limits, using the terrain elevation along the path for limits above ground. Between waypoints the altitude is taken to change evenly, so a leg which descends below a zone's ceiling only after passing it doesn't enter the zone. When the terrain or altitude is not known, the zone is assumed to apply: the terrain isn't interpolated across stretches without terrain data, so a zone with limits above ground counts as entered wherever the path passes over such a stretch.

The altitude profile below the Plan View map shades the restricted zones and U-space airspaces along the path between their floors and ceilings in the colors of the map, so you can see where to climb or descend. Limits above ground follow the terrain, and limits that are unknown or beyond the chart extend to its edges. Only the zones the path enters are labeled, the most restrictive first; labels which would overlap are stacked or left out. The distance axis is in km (or miles) for missions longer than 1 km (or 1 mile), with round steps.

If the vehicle supports a GeoFence, the warning also names the prohibited zones on the path which no exclusion fence covers, and **Add Exclusion Fences** adds them to the GeoFence so the vehicle stops at them. Fences added from a zone cover it whatever their simplification, while a fence which leaves out part of a zone, or a zone without area, doesn't count.

### Approximate Results {#approximate-results}

Where data is missing or a limit can only be estimated, the checks stay conservative, so the zone counts as entered, and say so: the mission check adds a line like "Approximate: Terrain unknown along part of the path, limits above ground taken as reached." to its warning, and the briefing lists the notes per zone and for the whole mission. The notes are:

| Note | JSON code | Meaning |
| ---- | --------- | ------- |
| Terrain unknown | `terrain_unknown` | Limits above ground were compared where the terrain elevation isn't known, so they were taken as reached. |
| Terrain sampled coarsely | `terrain_coarse` | The path is too long for the terrain positions (1000), which were spread farther apart than every 100 m. |
| Leg too long to follow its great circle | `great_circle_deviation` | A leg, like one across half the world, can't be checked within 1 m of its great circle. |
| Flight level limits | `flight_level` | Flight levels were converted with the QNH setting and widened by 4 %. |
| Zone schedule not fully known | `schedule_assumed` | The zone applies by a schedule which can't be fully evaluated, like one from an unknown daylight event, and was taken as active. |
| Altitude or its reference unknown | `altitude_reference_unknown` | A limit is in an unknown reference, or the altitude flown is unknown (like altitudes above home without the home altitude), so the limit was taken as reached. |

### Flight Time and Schedule Tolerance {#flight-time}

By default zone schedules are ignored, since the mission may be flown at another time. To check a flight at a known time, turn on **Check zones at the flight time** in the Plan View **Geozones** panel and enter the local start time (**Now** sets the current time). The mission check, the altitude profile and **Route Around Zones...** then leave out zones which aren't active while the mission flies through them, estimating when it does from its duration as if the mission is flown at an even pace. Since the take-off may be early or late and the pace may differ, a zone still counts when it is active within 15 minutes before or after the estimated times it is flown through. The warning shows the time and how many zones were left out.
The flight time belongs to the plan: it is saved with it, a plan opened without one has none, and a new plan, **Clear**, or a plan downloaded from the vehicle starts without one.

### Authorisations {#authorisations}

If you hold a permit for a zone, press **Add Authorisation** in its details and enter the permit number and, optionally, its first and last days in the form which opens in place, then **Save**. While the authorisation is valid, the zone is listed as authorised instead of warned about by the mission check, and the flight alerts, guided command checks and **Route Around Zones...** leave it out. With **Check zones at the flight time**, the authorisation must cover the whole flight; without it, the authorisation must be valid now, so a permit starting later still warns, noting when it starts (like "CTR: authorised from 2026-12-01."). Authorisations apply to the zone in every source, are listed in the **Authorisations** section of _Application Settings > Geozones_ where they can be removed, and are kept after a restart.

A **prohibited** zone can only be authorised with the permit number or reference and its last day: the form tells which one is missing, so a prohibited zone is never left out of the checks and alerts without a permit which can be referred to, nor for longer than it lasts. An authorisation of a prohibited zone without them, like one saved by an earlier version, added from a plan made elsewhere, or of a zone which became prohibited since, no longer counts: the zone is checked and alerted, and its details show the authorisation as "Not valid for a prohibited zone" with **Edit Authorisation** to complete it. Zones with other restrictions keep the optional permit number and days.

A permit is often limited. The optional scope fields of the form limit the authorisation to what the permit allows; left empty, it covers the whole zone at every altitude and time:

- **Ceiling**: the highest altitude allowed, in the units of the application, above ground when the zone has limits above ground and above mean sea level otherwise (the label says which). The mission check warns when the flight rises above it inside the zone (like "CTR: authorised up to 120 m AGL, the flight reaches 150 m AGL."), or when the height flown there isn't known, like above ground without terrain data. A ceiling of 99,999 m or more, the height published for zones without ceiling, isn't accepted: leave the field empty when the permit has none.
- **Daily hours**: the local times the flight must be within, like 08:00 and 18:00 (they can span midnight). They are checked for the whole flight with **Check zones at the flight time**, otherwise now.
- **Aircraft**: the registration or description of the aircraft, for information.
- **Only within the mission area**: limits the authorisation to the area of the mission planned in the Plan View, its bounds widened by 250 m. The mission check warns when the flight leaves it inside the zone.

For a prohibited zone the form marks **Permit** and **Last day** with \*: an authorisation for it needs both. When something is missing or can't be read, **Save** says what below the fields and the form stays open.

Flight alerts leave the zone out while the vehicle is within the area and below the ceiling; guided command checks leave it out when the path stays within them. **Route Around Zones...** goes around zones whose authorisation has a ceiling or an area. Authorisations saved before scopes existed, and those of plans without them, cover the whole zone.
An authorisation kept in the file or offered by a plan is left out when one of its dates, its ceiling, its area, its daily hours or its kind is there but can't be read, rather than applied without that limit: the zone is then checked and alerted as without authorisation.
The file of the authorisations (`authorisations.json` in the `GeoZones` folder of the application data location) is never replaced by less than it held: when it can't be read, no zone is authorised, a message says so and the file is moved to a backup next to it (`authorisations.json.<date>.bak`); when some of its authorisations are left out, it is copied to such a backup before the others are saved again; and a file saved by a newer version of _QGroundControl_ is neither read nor changed, so authorisations added then last until the application closes.
Saved plans keep the flight time, the authorisations of the zones the mission enters and the version of the geozone data (see [Plan Data Audit](#plan-data-audit)). When a plan with authorisations you don't have is opened, the Plan View banner lists them with **Add** and **Ignore**, so permits from someone else's plan are only added when you choose to.

#### Notifications {#notifications}

Some zone authorities only need to be notified before a flight, with a notice period (an ED-269 or ED-318 zone authority with the purpose `NOTIFICATION` and its `intervalBefore`, like `P2D`; an interval which isn't a duration, or is longer than 100 years, is taken as none, so the notice is due before the flight). The zone details show the notice, like "Notify Tower 2 d before the flight", and the mission check lists it for the zones the flight enters while they apply: with a planned start (see [Flight Time](#flight-time)) by when, like "CTR: notify Tower by 2026-06-13 10:00.", as a warning once that time has passed, otherwise how long before the flight. The briefing has it too. Once you notified the authority, press **Add Notification...** in the zone details and enter its reference and, optionally, the first and last days it is valid for: the mission check no longer asks for the notice while it is valid. A notification doesn't authorise the flight; it is listed with the authorisations, removed there or with **Remove Notification**, and kept with plans entering the zone like them.

### Return and Contingency Paths {#return-and-contingency-paths}

The return paths follow the parameters of the connected vehicle, read again whenever they, the rally points, the breach return point or the planned home position change:

- **PX4**: `RTL_TYPE` chooses the destination: 0 the closest of home and the rally points, 1 the closest rally point or mission landing (home without them), 3 the closest of home, the rally points and the mission landing, 5 the closest rally point (landing in place without one), 6 like 0 since the battery state is unknown. Types 2 and 4 continue the mission to its landing (no other path) or fly it in reverse and then home from the first waypoint after the takeoff, since PX4 lands at home instead of flying back to the takeoff. A destination must be 2 m closer than the next best one, like PX4 requires. The vehicle climbs to `RTL_RETURN_ALT` above the destination (or returns at its altitude when higher), descends to `RTL_DESCEND_ALT` at the destination and lands, or loiters there when `RTL_LAND_DELAY` is negative; fixed wings circle at `NAV_LOITER_RAD`. Multicopters lower the return altitude within `RTL_MIN_DIST` of the destination to the cone of `RTL_CONE_ANG`, at least twice `NAV_ACC_RAD` above it. A mission landing is a `DO_LAND_START` followed by a landing: the vehicle flies to its first waypoint, then the landing is part of the mission.
- **ArduCopter**: home or the closest rally point (`RALLY_INCL_HOME`, `RALLY_LIMIT_KM`). The vehicle climbs to `RTL_ALT` above home, at least `RTL_CLIMB_MIN` above its altitude and never lower than it (`RTL_ALT` 0 returns at the current altitude), limited by `RTL_CONE_SLOPE` near the destination and by the altitude fence (`FENCE_ALT_MAX` less `FENCE_MARGIN` when enabled), then descends to `RTL_ALT_FINAL` above home or lands. Both the centimeter parameters until Copter 4.6 and the meter parameters `RTL_ALT_M`, `RTL_CLIMB_MIN_M` and `RTL_ALT_FINAL_M` of later versions are read.
- **ArduPlane**: home at `RTL_ALTITUDE` above home (`ALT_HOLD_RTL` in centimeters on older versions, -1 for the current altitude) or the closest rally point at its altitude, climbing at once when lower and descending along the way when higher, then circling at `RTL_RADIUS` (or `WP_LOITER_RAD`). With `RTL_AUTOLAND` 1 the plane then flies the landing sequence of the closest `DO_LAND_START`, with 2 it flies to it directly, with 4 it joins the closest waypoint after a `DO_RETURN_PATH_START`.
- Other vehicles return straight to the closest of home and the rally points at their return altitude parameter.

Until the parameters of a connected vehicle are loaded, and without a connected vehicle, the check assumes the defaults of the firmware and vehicle class of the plan (**Vehicle Info** in the Mission Start item without a connected vehicle): `RTL_RETURN_ALT` 30 m and `RTL_DESCEND_ALT` 10 m for PX4 multicopters, `RTL_TYPE` 1, `RTL_RETURN_ALT` 100 m and loitering for PX4 fixed wings, `RTL_TYPE` 1 and `RTL_RETURN_ALT` 60 m for PX4 VTOLs (the airframe defaults, which differ from the parameter defaults of 0, 60 m and 30 m), `RTL_ALT` 15 m with `RTL_CONE_SLOPE` 3 for ArduCopter and `RTL_ALTITUDE` 100 m for ArduPlane, without rally limits or fences beyond their defaults. The warnings say so, like "assuming the PX4 default return altitude of 30 m". For other firmware the return altitude is unknown, zones along the way count at any altitude and the warnings say "at an unknown altitude".

The check also follows the contingency paths: the return the vehicle would fly from each item of the mission and from points every 250 m or more between them (at most 100 starts), after a failsafe or a fence breach. With ArduPlane's `FENCE_ACTION` 6 or 7 (guided) it also follows the flight to the breach return point of the GeoFence (home without one, the closest rally point with `FENCE_RET_RALLY`) at `FENCE_RET_ALT`, between the fence altitudes, or at the return altitude. Fence actions which return to launch (PX4 `GF_ACTION` 3, ArduPilot `FENCE_ACTION` 1) are covered by the returns, other actions hold, brake, land or retrace the path flown (SmartRTL) and add no path. Restricted zones only contingency paths enter are listed apart, one line per zone with the returns grouped by the items they would be flown from, like "Returns enter ...: a return to launch from items 3–7, 9". Unlike the planned flight they don't ask for confirmation on upload.

The return paths are estimates: wind, the turns and climb rate of fixed wings, terrain following returns (`RTL_ALT_TYPE`), PX4's geofence avoidance, SmartRTL, QuadPlane returns (`Q_RTL_MODE`), VTOL approach loiters and ArduPilot's checks of rally points against the fence aren't modeled. PX4 VTOLs are taken to return in fixed wing mode, without the cone, landing at the destination.

### Route Around Zones {#route-around-zones}

**Route Around Zones...** in the warning suggests, for each leg between waypoints which enters restricted zones, the ways to avoid them and recommends the cheapest one:

- **Detour**: waypoints around the zones keeping 100 m from them, which **Insert** adds to the mission at the altitudes the leg changes evenly through, which are those the detour was checked at, in the altitude mode of the new waypoints. When these altitudes can't be set, like above terrain which isn't loaded yet, no waypoint is inserted and the dialog tells why. The detour is the shortest path around the outlines of the zones, so it passes between zones, enters the bays of concave zones which are wider than 200 m and stays out of narrower ones, and stays within holes of zones. Waypoints within 100 m of a zone leave it without coming much closer. Detours longer than three times the leg, or 3 km longer when that is more, aren't suggested.
- **Climb**: raising the waypoints of the leg to 10 m above the ceilings of the zones, like "climb to 160 m above home over Zone X", when the leg then stays within the maximum height above the terrain set in the dialog (120 m by default).
- **Descent**: lowering the waypoints of the leg to 10 m below the floors of zones which don't reach the ground, as long as the leg stays at least 10 m above the terrain.

The cost of a detour is the distance it adds; a climb or a descent counts 8 m of distance per meter the waypoints move, for climbing and descending back. Climbs and descents need the altitudes of the leg and the terrain along it, and **Apply** changes waypoints with relative or AMSL altitudes, and those with altitudes above the terrain where the terrain at the waypoint is known. Each way is checked like the mission, at the altitudes of the leg over the terrain where its data is loaded, and must not enter other restricted zones. When a way isn't possible the dialog tells why, like "No detour: item 3 is inside Zone X.", "No climb: Zone X has no ceiling in meters." or "No descent: Zone X extends to the ground."; other reasons are zones surrounding the leg, detours too long, climbs above the maximum height and descents too close to the terrain. Among very many zones close together, like hundreds of small zones forming a maze, the detour may only be close to the shortest, and the search of a leg stops after a few seconds of work with "No detour: the search for a way around Zone X reached its limit."; a waypoint placed in a gap between the zones splits the search into shorter ones. The routes are searched in the background while the dialog shows the progress, **Cancel** stops the search and **Search Again** restarts it. The suggestions are for the mission and the zones they were searched with: a change of either removes them at once and starts a new search once the changes settle, and a suggestion is only applied to the waypoints it was made for. Check the new waypoints, altitudes, the terrain and obstacles before flying.

Each way is one line, and a leg without any way around its zones one line like "Leg to item 5: no route avoids ...". **Why ▾** shows why the other ways don't work. When only the maximum height keeps the leg from climbing over the zones, **Allow Climb to ...** raises the maximum to the height the climb needs and searches again. The maximum is shown rounded to whole units in **Climb at most to**; it only changes when you type another number there.

### Pre-flight Briefing {#pre-flight-briefing}

**Export ▾** > **Geozone Briefing...** in the geozone banner above the Plan View map saves a briefing of the geozones of the planned mission. The file dialog proposes the folder and name of the open plan with `-briefing.pdf`; the extension of the file name chooses the format:

- **PDF** (`.pdf`): an A4 document to print or take along, with page numbers. It is printed in dark text on white whatever the color theme of the desktop, and its text can be searched and copied.
- **Web page** (`.html`): a single file without external resources, which reads well on a phone and prints cleanly, in dark text on white also in browsers with a dark theme.
- **JSON** (`.json`): the same content for other tools, see below.

The briefing lists the mission (vehicle and firmware, planned start and estimated end, duration, distance, highest altitude, home and how the returns were modeled), then:

- **Zones the flight path enters**, in the order the mission enters them: where (after which item, how far along the mission, the estimated times with a planned start), the altitudes flown inside against the zone floor and ceiling at the entry, whether the result is approximate (see [Approximate Results](#approximate-results)), when the zone is active during the flight (widened by 15 minutes like the mission check) and its published schedule, the vertical limits as published, the authority and its contacts, the message and conditions, U-space class, exemptions, your authorisation with whether it covers the flight, and the source and content hash of the zone.
- **Zones only returns would enter**, with the returns and the items they would be flown from.
- **Restricted zones within 500 m** of the flight path, or passed above or below it, with their distance.
- **Data versions**: each source covering the mission with its format, file or feed, data (issue) date, the AIRAC cycle in effect at that date (not for live restrictions), its published validity, when it was last checked, and the hash of its content; the JSON file adds its provenance.

The JSON file has `"schema": "qgc-geozone-briefing"` and `"version": 1`. The version increases when fields are removed or change meaning, not when fields are added. Times are ISO 8601 UTC, distances and altitudes meters (altitudes above mean sea level), unknown values `null`. The fields are:

- `generated`, `generator`, `scheduleToleranceSeconds`, `nearbyDistanceMeters`, `approximations` (codes of the [approximate results](#approximate-results) of the mission and its returns).
- `mission`: `name`, `planFile`, `vehicle`, `firmware`, `plannedStart`, `plannedEnd`, `durationSeconds`, `distanceMeters`, `maxAltitudeAMSL`, `maxAltitudeAboveHome`, `home` (`lat`, `lon`, `altitudeAMSL`), `itemCount`, `returnAssumptions` (texts).
- `zones`: `involvement` (`entered`, `contingency` or `nearby`), `key`, `identifier`, `country`, `name`, `restriction` (`prohibited`, `req_authorisation`, `conditional`, `no_restriction`, `u_space` or `unknown`), `restrictionCode` (as published), `restrictionText`, `reasons`, `verticalLimits`, `schedule`, `activeDuringFlight`, `activePeriods` (`start`, `end`), `crossings` (`path`: empty for the flight path or what the vehicle does, like "the loiter at item 3", `entryItem` and `exitItem`: sequence number of the last item reached before, `entryDistance`, `exitDistance`, `entry` and `exit` (`lat`, `lon`), `entryTime`, `exitTime`, `pathAltitudeMin`, `pathAltitudeMax`, `zoneFloor`, `zoneCeiling`, `active`), `inArea`, `contingencies` (texts), `distance`, `passed` (`above`, `below` or `null`), `authorities` (texts), `message`, `conditions`, `otherReasonInfo`, `uSpaceClass`, `regulationExemption`, `authorisation` (`permit`, `validFrom`, `validTo`, `status`, `coversFlight`, or `null`), `notification` (the notice the zone authority needs or the notification given, or `null`), `source`, `contentHash` (hexadecimal) and `approximations` (codes).
- `operator` and `aircraft`, `null` unless [operator details](#operator-details) are included: `operator` has `id`, `idType` and `region` (`FAA` or `EU`), `aircraft` has `uasId`, `uasIdType`, `uaType`, `category` and `class` (EU), `null` when not set.
- `sources`: `key`, `name`, `format`, `provider`, `dataDate`, `loaded`, `checked`, `validFrom`, `validTo`, `airacCycle`, `contentHash` (hexadecimal) and `provenance` (see [Provenance and Offline Packs](#provenance-and-offline-packs)).

### Operational Intent {#operational-intent}

**Export ▾** > **Operational Intent...** in the geozone banner above the Plan View map saves the operational intent of the planned mission as JSON, for a flight authorisation request to a U-space service provider (USSP) or a UTM service supplier (USS): 4D volumes which together contain the flight path while the vehicle is expected to fly it. The file dialog proposes the folder and name of the open plan with `-intent.json`. It needs the flight time: turn on **Check zones at the flight time** in the **Geozones** panel first (see [Flight Time](#flight-time)), and the flight must not be over.

The volumes contain the flight path with a horizontal buffer of 50 m around it and a vertical buffer below and above it (20 m), from a time buffer before the vehicle is estimated at each place until the time buffer after it (15 minutes, like the schedule tolerance of the mission check). The times are estimated from the mission duration, the flight path includes the loops of `DO_JUMP` items and the return to launch of the mission, and survey patterns are covered by their flight lines. Legs close together, like those of a survey or the short legs of a pattern, share a volume; long legs are cut into volumes of their own, so that the airspace isn't reserved along the whole route for the whole flight; and when there would be more volumes than the maximum (50) the legs adding the least airspace are merged. Each volume is a polygon (or a circle for a hover in place) widened by 1 % for the differences between the earth models, its altitudes are heights above the WGS84 ellipsoid, converted from the mission altitudes with the EGM96 geoid, rounded outward to whole meters, and its times are rounded outward to whole seconds.

There is no public JSON schema of the EU U-space flight authorisation service: Regulation (EU) 2021/664 lists the content of a request in its Annex IV but leaves the format to the service providers. The volumes therefore follow the data model of the ASTM F3548-21 standard for UTM strategic coordination, which U-space and UTM implementations share: the `OperationalIntentDetails` and `Volume4D` schemas of its API definition, [utm.yaml](https://github.com/astm-utm/Protocol/blob/master/utm.yaml), which the [InterUSS](https://github.com/interuss) implementations use. The file has `"schema": "qgc-operational-intent"` and `"version": 1`, which increases when fields are removed or change meaning, not when fields are added. Its fields are:

- `standard` (`ASTM F3548-21`), `generatedAt`, `generator`, `notes` (texts, like when the mission repeats without end and the volumes cover one pass).
- `flight`: `name`, `planFile`, `vehicle`, `plannedStart`, `plannedEnd`, `distanceMeters`, `repeatsIndefinitely`. Times are ISO 8601 UTC.
- `operator` and `aircraft`, like those of the [briefing](#pre-flight-briefing), `null` unless [operator details](#operator-details) are included.
- `parameters`: `horizontalBufferMeters`, `verticalBufferMeters`, `timeBufferSeconds`, `maxVolumes`.
- `uSpaceAirspaces`: the U-space airspaces the flight path enters while they apply, where the flight needs the authorisation: `key`, `identifier`, `country`, `name`.
- `authorisations`: your [authorisations](#authorisations) of the zones the flight path enters: `zoneKey`, `zoneName`, `permit`, `validFrom`, `validTo`. Enter the authorisation or approval number the authority or service provider returns as the permit of the zone's authorisation, so that the next export and the briefing reference it.
- `operational_intent`, an ASTM F3548-21 `OperationalIntent` without the fields the USS and the DSS assign (`id`, `manager`, `version`, `ovn`, `uss_base_url`, `subscription_id`, `uss_availability`):
  - `reference`: `state` (`Accepted`, the state requested for a planned flight), `time_start` and `time_end` of the whole flight with the buffers.
  - `details` (`OperationalIntentDetails`): `volumes`, `off_nominal_volumes` (empty, since they only apply to flights which became nonconforming or contingent) and `priority` (0, the regulator defines the priorities).
  - Each volume (`Volume4D`) has `time_start` and `time_end` (`Time`: `value` in RFC 3339 UTC and `format` `RFC3339`) and `volume` (`Volume3D`) with either `outline_polygon` (`vertices`: `lat`, `lng`, not closed) or `outline_circle` (`center`: `lat`, `lng`; `radius`: `value`, `units` `M`), and `altitude_lower` and `altitude_upper` (`value`, `reference` `W84`, `units` `M`).

The export doesn't submit the operational intent: _QGroundControl_ doesn't connect to a USS, USSP or DSS, doesn't check the volumes against other operational intents or constraints, and doesn't receive the approval. Submit the file, or its volumes, through the service of your provider. Of the operator and aircraft details of an EU flight authorisation request, the operator registration number and the serial number, type, category and class of the aircraft are included from the Remote ID settings when you choose to (see [Operator Details](#operator-details)); the mode of the operation, identification and connectivity, endurance and emergency procedure aren't known to _QGroundControl_. The volumes use the altitudes of the mission check: missions with altitudes relative to home take the altitude of the planned home position, which comes from the terrain data, so check it in the **Launch Position** of the Mission Start item when the terrain data isn't available. Returns after a failsafe or a fence breach aren't part of the volumes.

### Operator Details {#operator-details}

With **Include operator details in exports** on (_Application Settings > Geozones > Records_, off by default), the briefings, operational intents and [flight records](#flight-records) include the operator and aircraft of the _Remote ID_ settings: the operator ID and its type, the Remote ID region, the basic ID of the aircraft (serial number or registration) with its type, the aircraft type and, with the EU classification, its category and class. Turn it on only when the files go to the authorities or service providers that need them.

### Plan Data Audit {#plan-data-audit}

When a plan is saved while geozone data is loaded, the plan file keeps, in the `audit` object of its `geoZones` section, the version of each source covering the mission (data date and content hash) and the content hash of each zone the mission or a return from along it enters, whatever its schedule.
When the plan is opened again, and whenever the sources reload while it is open, the zones are compared with the current data. If they differ, the banner above the map warns, like "Geozone data changed since this plan was saved: 2 zone(s) modified, 1 new. Review the plan.":

- **modified**: a zone of the plan whose published content (geometry, limits, schedule, restriction, name...) changed. Zones are matched by their source, identifier, name and restriction, or by their country and identifier when they were renamed, reclassified or moved to another source.
- **removed**: a zone of the plan no source has anymore, or whose source was removed or disabled.
- **new**: a zone the mission now enters which came with updated or newly loaded data. Zones the mission enters only because you edited it don't count.
- **source updated** or **removed**: the data of a source covering the mission changed, even when the zones of the mission didn't.

**Details** lists the changes and **Acknowledge** hides the warning, and the confirmation it asks for on upload, until the data changes again. Saving the plan records the current data, so the warning disappears. Plans saved without this record, like older plans, open without warning, and so do plans whose record was written by a newer version of QGroundControl in a format this version doesn't know, which it doesn't read.

### My Zones {#my-zones}

Operators can draw zones of their own in the Plan View, like a quarry during blasting, an event or a customer's site, which are then shown, checked by the mission check, alerted in flight, offered by **From Geozones...** and exported in packs like the zones of any source.
In the **Geozones** panel, **My zones** lists them with their restriction color: **Draw Polygon** or **Draw Circle** starts a zone in the middle of the map, **Edit** changes one and **Remove** deletes it.

While a zone is drawn, the map shows its shape with the polygon and circle editing of the GeoFence (drag the corners, the **+** between them to add one, the center, or the circle's edge; the polygon's menu also traces or loads KML), and a form at the bottom left asks for its **Name**, **Restriction** (**Prohibited**, **Authorisation required**, **Conditional** or **No restriction**), **Floor** and **Ceiling** in meters above the ground (`AGL`) or sea level (`AMSL`), empty for the ground and no ceiling, and an optional **Message**. **Save** keeps the zone, **Cancel** drops the changes. A zone has no schedule: it is always active.

The zones are kept in the source **My zones**, an ED-318 file (`operator/my-zones.json` in the geozone storage folder) whose zones have the reason `OTHER` ("Drawn by the operator") and the authority "Operator". Removing the last zone, or the source in _Application Settings > Geozones_, deletes the file; removing another source never does. Turning the source off hides the zones without deleting them.

When the file can't be read, like after a disk error, **My zones** tells so: its zones are neither shown nor checked until the file is repaired. The file is never overwritten: the next zone you draw is saved to a new file and the unreadable one is kept beside it as `my-zones.unreadable-<date>.json`. When a zone can't be removed because the file can't be deleted or emptied, **My zones** tells why and the zone stays listed, shown and checked.

### Add Geozones to the GeoFence {#add-geozones-to-the-geofence}

If the vehicle supports GeoFences, loaded zones can be converted into exclusion fences which fit into the vehicle's fence storage. Press **From Geozones...** below **Polygon Fence** and **Circular Fence** in the [GeoFence](../plan_view/plan_geofence.md) panel, then in the dialog:

1. Check the zone types to add (**Prohibited**, **Authorisation**, **Conditional**).
1. Leave **Only zones in current map view** checked to only add the zones visible on the map (recommended for national files that contain many zones).
1. Select when the zones must be **Active**: **At flight time** (offered and selected when the plan has a planned start: from the planned start to the estimated end of the mission, widened by 15 minutes), **Any time**, **Now**, or within the **Next 3 hours** or **Next 24 hours**. Except for **Any time**, only zones active during the whole of that time are fenced, see [Time-limited Zones](#time-limited-zones-and-fences).
1. Select how far fences may be simplified to fit (**Simplify up to**). Fences are only simplified as far as needed, the most important zones the least. Simplified fences always enclose the whole zone and extend up to twice the tolerance beyond it. Select **Off** to keep the published vertices.
1. Check the zones selected and, with **Show**, those left out, then press **Apply** to add the fences to the plan, or **Upload to Vehicle**.

**Help** shows the explanations of the dialog. The selected zones are listed first, then the zones left out under a line like _193 geozones NOT fenced, 2 covered by other fences_ in the warning color, listed in gray except prohibited zones the planned flight enters, which stay red. **Hide** collapses that list.

The dialog shows how much of the vehicle's fence the selection uses, like _Vehicle fence: 52 of 64 points_:

- **PX4** stores 64 fence items: one per polygon vertex, circle and breach return point.
- **ArduPilot** (4.0 and later) stores the fence in a storage area of the flight controller: each polygon takes 2 bytes plus 8 bytes per vertex, each circle 13 bytes and the return point 9 bytes, and a polygon has at most 255 vertices. On flight controllers with 16 KB of storage, like the Pixhawk and Cube, Copter (and Heli) has 560 bytes (70 items) and Plane, Rover and Sub have 672 bytes (84 items). Flight controllers with less storage hold fewer points; a rejected upload is reported. When `BRD_SD_FENCE` stores the fence on the SD card, its size is used instead. `FENCE_TOTAL` only counts the points of the last upload and is not a limit.
- Without a connected vehicle the fence is fitted to what both PX4 and ArduPilot copters store (64 points, 560 bytes) and circles are added as polygons enclosing them.

The fence budget is a hard limit of the autopilot, not of _QGroundControl_: PX4 rejects a fence of more than 64 items (`MAV_MISSION_NO_SPACE`), ArduPilot rejects more items than its storage holds (`MAV_MISSION_NO_SPACE`, "Only N items are supported") and fences which need more bytes than its storage has (`MAV_MISSION_ERROR`, "Fence exceeds storage size"). PX4's limit is fixed in its firmware (`DM_KEY_FENCE_POINTS_MAX`); ArduPilot's depends on the flight controller and isn't reported, so _QGroundControl_ estimates it unless the fence is on the SD card. When the vehicle rejects an upload, the dialog says why, the fence on the vehicle stays as it was and the plan's fence stays marked as changed: leave out zones or allow more simplification and upload again.

The panel warns when the vehicle won't enforce exclusion fences: ArduPilot with `FENCE_TYPE` lacking polygon fences or `FENCE_ENABLE` at 0, PX4 with `GF_ACTION` set to None.

Zones are selected in priority order until the fence is full: prohibited zones first, then other restricted zones (authorisation required, conditional and unknown), then the others. Within those, zones the planned flight enters (between their altitude limits) come first, then those within 1 km of the flight, then the farther ones by distance, then those with fewer fence points. Fences already added from geozones are replaced, while drawn fences and the breach return point are kept and take room. The left out zones are listed (the first 500 of them) with the reason: no room left, too many points even simplified, the same area as another zone, or left out by you.

The checkbox of a zone means _include it if it fits_. Checked zones are included in priority order while they fit, so a checked zone can be left out when there is no room for it. Unchecked zones are left out by you. Uncheck a zone to leave it out, which makes room for the zones after it, or check a zone left out by you to include it again in its place in the priority order. The lists change as soon as you click: a zone moves between **Selected zones** and **Left out**, and zones after it may follow when the room changes. Zones without a fence of their own can't be checked: those without an area, those when the vehicle doesn't support exclusion fences, and those with the same area as a zone before them in priority, which follow that zone: they are covered by its fence when it is selected and left out with it otherwise, so unchecking a zone leaves out its area. Hover over a row to see its whole name and reason when they don't fit.

Zones the flight only passes above or below are left out unless you check them, since their exclusion fence would stop the vehicle there.

#### Time-limited Zones and Fences {#time-limited-zones-and-fences}

A vehicle fence has no schedule: once uploaded, the vehicle enforces it all the time. A fence of a zone active only part of the flight would make the vehicle act at a zone which isn't active yet, or when the zone becomes active while the vehicle is inside it, where EN 4709-003 §6.2.1 asks for a warning but no automatic manoeuvre. So the import only fences zones active during the whole planned time: with **At flight time** from the planned start to the estimated end widened by 15 minutes, with **Next 3 hours** and **Next 24 hours** from now to then, and with **Now** the zones active now. A zone active only part of that time, like one starting or ending during the flight, is left out with the reason _Active only part of the flight, QGroundControl warns instead_ and can't be checked: the [flight alerts](#flight-alerts) follow its schedule and warn when it becomes active. A zone with the same area which is active all the time is fenced in its place. **Any time** fences the zones whatever their schedule, also while they are inactive.

#### Zones Not Fenced {#zones-not-fenced}

The vehicle doesn't stop at a zone which is not fenced; _QGroundControl_ still [alerts](#flight-alerts) about it. The zones not fenced are all those left out except the zones with the same area as a selected zone, which its fence covers. They are named, with why, wherever the fence is used:

- in the dialog, in the warning color above the list of zones left out;
- before **Upload to Vehicle**, which asks for confirmation listing them;
- in the GeoFence panel of the Plan View, like "3 geozones are not fenced: A (No room left in the vehicle fence), B (Left out by you) and 1 more.", with **Not Fenced...** listing all of them, and in the mission banner (_N left out of the fence_, listed under **Details**). The plan file keeps the list (up to 100 zones and their count), so it is shown again when the plan is opened. It is shown until the fences from geozones change: editing or deleting them, or applying another selection, which then gives its own list;
- when uploading the plan, whose confirmation lists them;
- in the message of a verified upload, and in the **Fence** item of the [pre-flight checklist](#pre-flight-checklist), as a warning to accept.

**Upload to Vehicle** adds the selected fences to the plan and uploads the plan's fence (not the mission) like a plan upload, then reads the fence back from the vehicle and compares it with the plan: the number of polygons and circles, each vertex and circle center within 0.5 m, the circle radii and the breach return point. The dialog shows whether the vehicle stores the fence as planned, the differences, or why the vehicle rejected it, for example when it has no room for it.

The upload is aborted when the plan's fence is edited or another plan is opened (or the plan is saved under another name) before it is verified, since the vehicle wouldn't store the fence of the plan: the fence stays marked as changed, so upload it again. It fails when the vehicle disconnects meanwhile.

Fences added from geozones are named after their zones and listed separately in the GeoFence panel, where **Delete Geozone Fences** deletes them (see [Edit/Delete a GeoFence](../plan_view/plan_geofence.md#edit-delete-a-geofence)).

Each fence from a geozone also keeps the zone's key, the content hash of its data and the end of its last active period, and the plan keeps the settings of the dialog the fences were selected with (zone types, area, active time, simplification and the zones you checked or unchecked), in keys other ground stations ignore. When the zone of a fence changed since, is no longer loaded or its last period ended, the GeoFence panel warns, like "Geozone fences are outdated: A (changed), TMP (expired).", and uploading the plan asks for confirmation. **Rebuild Geozone Fences** replaces the fences from geozones with a selection made again with the same settings (for plans saved without them, the zones of the fences still loaded, simplified up to 50 m). Fences of plans saved before these keys existed aren't checked.

::: warning
GeoFences have no altitude limits, so a zone's vertical limits are not transferred and the whole zone is excluded at all altitudes.
Holes in zone polygons are also dropped. The ArduPilot fence storage is estimated, so verify the upload.
:::

## Flight {#flight}

### Pre-flight Checklist {#pre-flight-checklist}

With the pre-flight checklist turned on (_Application Settings > Fly View > Use Preflight Checklist_), the checklist of the vehicle type ends with a **Geozones** group whose items check themselves for the active vehicle and its mission:

- **Geozone data**: zones are loaded and no source is [outdated](#managing-sources), or expired when outdated data is treated as invalid.
- **Mission**: the mission on the vehicle enters no restricted zone, or only zones with an [authorisation](#authorisations) covering the [planned start](#flight-time) (at any time without one). A prohibited zone fails the check.
- **Fence**: the vehicle's fence is the one an upload of the [fence import](#add-geozones-to-the-geofence) verified, its fences from geozones were made from data which hasn't changed since, and it excludes the prohibited zones the mission enters. A fence uploaded otherwise is reported as not verified. The [zones the import left out](#zones-not-fenced) are listed as a warning. After a fence upload the vehicle rejected, or a fence download which failed, the item warns that the fence on the vehicle is unknown, rather than telling there is none, until the fence was read back. It also warns while the fence is [disabled by the pilot](#override-fence-action).
- **QNH**: limits published as flight levels within 1 km of the mission and 300 m of its altitudes are converted with a [QNH](#settings-reference) other than the standard pressure.
- **Remote ID**: when the mission enters U-space airspace, the operator ID is set (and valid in the EU) in _Application Settings > Remote ID_ and the vehicle's Remote ID reports it is ready to arm.
- **Take-off position**: the vehicle on the ground isn't inside a restricted zone (see [On the Ground Inside a Zone](#on-the-ground)). Inside a prohibited zone the item fails; inside another restricted zone it warns until you confirm the permission to fly there.
- **Geo-awareness**: [geo-awareness](#geo-awareness) is available for the vehicle; it warns with the reasons when it is degraded or unavailable.
- **Permits**: you acknowledged the [permit to enter](#permits) each zone requiring an authorisation or a notification which the vehicle is in or the mission enters (zones with a valid authorisation aren't listed). The zones are listed below the item, each with **I Have Permission** to acknowledge it for this flight.

Each item shows its state and why: green when it passed or doesn't apply, orange for a warning, which you can accept by clicking it, and red for a failure, which you can't. You accept the warning shown: when its text changes, like from a missing position to a zone the vehicle is in, the item is orange again. The reset button of the checklist and a change of the active vehicle also reset the geozone group, so warnings accepted before are to be accepted again. The checklist passes when the vehicle checklist and the geozone group pass, so with _Enforce Preflight Checklist_ a failed geozone item prevents arming.

### Flight Alerts {#flight-alerts}

While flying, _QGroundControl_ warns once (with a message and audio) when a connected vehicle comes within the [alert thresholds](#alert-thresholds) of an active prohibited, authorisation required, conditional, unknown or U-space zone, horizontally or below its floor or above its ceiling, or would enter it within the warning time at its current speed, direction and climb rate, and again when it enters the zone. Alerts based on the velocity give the time to entry, so fast vehicles are warned in time. The approach distances are never shorter than 6 seconds at the vehicle's current speed (300 m at 50 m/s), so with the checks every second the pilot gets at least 5 seconds of warning also when the entry isn't predicted, for example while the vehicle turns. The velocity is the one the vehicle sends with its position, or is estimated from its positions when it sends none. Limits above ground use the vehicle's height above terrain, and the predicted path and guided commands use the terrain ahead where its data is loaded, so rising ground towards a zone counts. Where it isn't loaded, the terrain ahead is unknown rather than taken from the last known position, so zones with limits above ground count as reaching any altitude there. When the vehicle sends no height above terrain, its height above ground is estimated from the terrain data of _QGroundControl_, which is less accurate: the altitude strip shows an **AGL estimated** badge and the indicator says so. Without terrain data for the vehicle position, zones with limits above ground are assumed to apply at any altitude once the vehicle is armed; a disarmed vehicle is taken to be on the ground.

While a vehicle's link or position is lost, its alerts pause, since its last position isn't where it is. One alert names the most restrictive zone it was in or near: "Position lost inside geozone ..." or "Position lost near geozone ...". Its nearby zones and altitude strip keep showing its last position, marked **Stale** (a **?** badge on the indicator), and "Still inside geozone ..." isn't repeated meanwhile. When the position is back, the alerts resume: a vehicle still inside a prohibited zone is reminded at once, and one which left it is no longer.

The position is lost when the vehicle hasn't sent the message its position on the map comes from for 5 seconds: `GLOBAL_POSITION_INT` with a position (ArduPilot sends it at 0/0 without one), `GPS_RAW_INT` with a 3D fix for vehicles which never sent the former, or a high latency message. Other messages don't count, so a position frozen on the map is reported as lost also while the receiver still sends its status. On slow links, like satellite ones, the position is lost after three of the usual intervals of that message (the median of the last five, so one message after an outage isn't taken for a slow link), and after a minute at the latest.

Every connected vehicle is monitored on its own, and alerts name the vehicle when several are connected. A vehicle which reconnects starts afresh, even with the same system id, so its alerts repeat. The geozone indicator in the Fly View toolbar takes the color of the active vehicle's [alert level](#alert-levels) while it is near or inside a zone, and is dimmed while no vehicle is monitored. Click it to see the nearby zones and, when it is checked, the [traffic](#traffic) in or near zones; expand it to change the display, spoken alert, extra warning and traffic settings.

Flight alerts and the [alert log](#alert-log) are always on and can't be turned off, since EN 4709-003 requires the warnings to be given: you only choose whether they are spoken (**Speak flight alerts**). Settings of earlier versions which turned them off are ignored.

### Zones Becoming Active {#zone-activation}

A [caution](#alert-levels) gives the pilot time to leave before a zone applies: when a time-limited zone the vehicle is inside, or within its [approach distances](#alert-thresholds) of, becomes active within **Caution before zones become active** (10 minutes by default), the alert says when, like "Geozone ... becomes active in 9 min, you are inside it" or "Geozone ... nearby becomes active in 40 s". Each activation of a zone is cautioned once (the next day's activation of a daily schedule is cautioned again), and logged as `activation_soon` with the seconds left in `time_to_entry_s`.

When a zone becomes active while a vehicle is inside it (its schedule starts, new data adds or changes it, or an authorisation for it ends), _QGroundControl_ raises a [warning](#alert-levels) "Geozone ... is now active — you are inside it. Leave or land as soon as possible.", asks for the [permission](#permission-question) and, for a prohibited zone, repeats it like an entry. As EN 4709-003 §6.2.1 requires, this is only a warning: _QGroundControl_ never sends a manoeuvre by itself, so the pilot decides how to leave or land (the popup's **Hold**, **Return** and **Climb Above Ceiling** only open the usual confirmation slider). The vehicle's own fence doesn't change either: fences built from geozones with **From Geozones...** are static, uploaded before the flight, and time-limited zones aren't fenced (see [Add Geozones to the GeoFence](#add-geozones-to-the-geofence)), so a zone becoming active never triggers the vehicle's fence action. A vehicle taking off inside an active zone gets the usual "Entered geozone ..." alert.

The alerts follow the schedules of the zones around each vehicle at every evaluation, once a second: the warning comes within a second of the zone's start, the approach and entry alerts apply from then, and the map draws the zone as active at the same time. Zones away from the vehicles are looked at every minute.

### Alert Levels {#alert-levels}

Alerts have a level after EN 4709-003 Annex D, shown by its color and word in the alert popup, the indicator, the predicted path on the map and the **Recent Alerts**, spoken first ("Warning: ...", "Caution: ...") and recorded in the [alert log](#alert-log):

| Level | Color | When |
| ----- | ----- | ---- |
| **WARNING** | Red | The vehicle is inside a prohibited, authorisation required or unknown zone without your approval, its entry into one is predicted within the reaction time (plus the 1 s between the checks), or a zone became active around it. The vehicle's own fence breaches. |
| **CAUTION** | Amber | The vehicle approaches such a zone, is in or near U-space airspace, a zone becomes active soon, or its position is lost. |
| **ADVISORY** | Neutral | Conditional zones, zones you approved (**I do**), traffic entering zones. |

So a vehicle flying towards a restricted zone, horizontally or climbing or descending into it, gets a caution when it comes within the [alert thresholds](#alert-thresholds), a warning when the entry is imminent and a warning when it enters, each shown and spoken. Each alert in the popup has a bar and the word of its level, and the popup's frame takes the color of the highest level shown; its background stays dark so that it is readable over the zone colors of the map. While the vehicle is in a zone, the **Zone altitudes** strip (or its chip) is framed in the level's color too. The zone fills of the map keep the colors of their restriction (see the legend), which tell the kind of zone rather than how urgent an alert is.

### On the Ground Inside a Zone {#on-the-ground}

A connected vehicle which is disarmed is on the ground. When its position puts it inside an active restricted zone, at connection or when it gets a position fix, _QGroundControl_ alerts it at once, like a pre-arm message of the vehicle, shown, spoken and logged (event `on_ground`):

- inside a prohibited zone, a warning: "On the ground inside prohibited geozone ... — take-off not allowed here", reminded every 30 seconds like a vehicle inside a prohibited zone;
- inside a zone requiring an authorisation, a caution: "On the ground inside geozone ... (Authorisation required) — take-off only with an authorisation", and for conditional or unknown zones "— check its conditions before take-off".

Zones you hold a valid [authorisation](#authorisations) for aren't alerted. The alert asks **Do you have legal permission to fly here?** like the [flight alerts](#permission-question). When the vehicle is armed, or acknowledges an arm command it refuses (for example after its own pre-arm checks failed), the alert is raised again as "Arming inside ..." (event `on_ground` with the detail `arming`), once per attempt, unless you confirmed the permission. This works for PX4 and ArduPilot alike; arming from the remote control is alerted when the vehicle reports being armed. The confirmations of **Arm**, **Takeoff** and **Start Mission** in the Fly View show the same warning, and the [pre-flight checklist](#pre-flight-checklist) item **Take-off position** fails inside a prohibited zone. The geozone indicator shows a red (prohibited) or orange (other restricted zones) **✕** badge, and its page starts with the warning. _QGroundControl_ can't prevent the vehicle from arming: use the fence or the pre-arm checks of the vehicle for that.

A vehicle whose armed state isn't known, like positions replayed from logs, is treated as flying.

### Geo-Awareness Status {#geo-awareness}

Geo-awareness only works with current zone data and a good position of the vehicle (EN 4709-003 §5.2.3). _QGroundControl_ checks both every second for the active vehicle and shows the result at the top of the geozone indicator page: **Available**, **Degraded** (orange) or **Unavailable** (red), with the reasons:

| Reason | State |
| --- | --- |
| Telemetry link lost | Unavailable |
| No current vehicle position (none received, or none for 5 seconds) | Unavailable |
| GNSS lost: the vehicle reports no fix (`GPS_RAW_INT`) | Unavailable |
| No geozone data loaded | Unavailable |
| GNSS poor: a 2D fix, fewer than 6 satellites, HDOP above 5 or a horizontal accuracy worse than 5 m | Degraded |
| Geozone data expired, or may be outdated (see [Managing Sources](#managing-sources)) | Degraded |
| The last update of a source failed, like a feed download | Degraded |
| No source has zones around the vehicle position (by the bounds of its zones, or the area of a service) | Degraded |

The GNSS limits are set by the manufacturer for each vehicle type in the [alert profiles](#alert-thresholds) (`minSatellites`, `maxHdop`, `maxHorizontalAccuracy`); the vehicle's own GNSS checks apply as well. When geo-awareness isn't available, the geozone indicator shows a **!** badge, orange when degraded and red when unavailable, and is dimmed when unavailable; the indicator is shown whenever a vehicle is connected, also without zones. The [pre-flight checklist](#pre-flight-checklist) has a **Geo-awareness** item.

While a vehicle is armed, a change of state that lasts (2 seconds, 5 seconds when it gets better) is alerted once, shown, spoken and logged (event `geo_awareness`, with the state as the zone identifier and the reasons as its name): a warning when it becomes unavailable ("Geo-awareness unavailable: telemetry link lost"), a caution when it is degraded ("Geo-awareness degraded: GNSS poor (2D fix)"), and an advisory when it is available again ("Geo-awareness available again"). Arming without geo-awareness is alerted the same way. On the ground only the indicator and the checklist show it.

### Alert Thresholds {#alert-thresholds}

How early a vehicle is warned follows EN 4709-003 Annex A: early enough that the pilot can still keep the vehicle out of the zone after reacting. The thresholds are defined by the manufacturer for each vehicle type (multirotor, fixed wing, VTOL, and a default for other types, from the MAVLink vehicle type) and computed every second from the vehicle's actual ground speed _v_, vertical speed _w_ and position accuracy:

- Horizontal distance: _D_ = max(minimum distance, K·σ + v·(t<sub>r</sub> + 1 s) + manoeuvre span + wind·(t<sub>r</sub> + t<sub>m</sub>) + safety margin) + extra distance + v·extra time.
- Vertical distance below a floor or above a ceiling: the same with the vertical position error, w, the distance to stop the climb or descent (w²/2a<sub>v</sub>), the vertical wind and the vertical margin, at least the minimum vertical distance, + w·extra time.
- Warning time before a predicted entry: t<sub>r</sub> + t<sub>m</sub> + 1 s + the margins (K·σ, wind drift and safety margin) along the direction of travel at the current speed, at most 120 s, + extra time.

K·σ is the position uncertainty: σ is the horizontal accuracy the vehicle reports with its GNSS position (`GPS_RAW_INT` `h_acc`, vertically `v_acc`), else its HDOP times the range error (UERE), else a default. t<sub>r</sub> is the reaction time (a pilot by default, the profile also has the shorter time of an automatic reaction), the manoeuvre span and time t<sub>m</sub> are the braking distance v²/2a and time v/a, or for fixed wing aircraft the turn radius v²/(g·tan φ) and the time of a quarter turn, and 1 s is the interval between the checks. So a vehicle flying straight towards a zone is warned at least the reaction time plus the manoeuvre time before it would enter it, horizontally and when climbing or descending into it, and poor GNSS warns earlier.

The default profiles, which are conservative:

| Value | Default | Multirotor | Fixed wing | VTOL |
| ----- | ------- | ---------- | ---------- | ---- |
| K (position error factor) | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |
| Range error (UERE) / default position error σ | 5 m / 10 m | 5 m / 10 m | 5 m / 10 m | 5 m / 10 m |
| Vertical error (without `v_acc`) | 1.5·σ | 1.5·σ | 1.5·σ | 1.5·σ |
| Reaction time, pilot / automatic | 5 s / 3 s | 5 s / 3 s | 5 s / 3 s | 5 s / 3 s |
| Manoeuvre | brake 2 m/s² | brake 2.5 m/s² | turn at 30° bank | brake 1.5 m/s² |
| Vertical deceleration | 1.5 m/s² | 2 m/s² | 1.5 m/s² | 1.5 m/s² |
| Wind / vertical wind | 10 / 1 m/s | 8 / 1 m/s | 12 / 2 m/s | 10 / 1 m/s |
| Safety margin, horizontal / vertical | 20 m / 10 m | 10 m / 10 m | 20 m / 10 m | 20 m / 10 m |
| Minimum distance, horizontal / vertical | 100 m / 30 m | 100 m / 30 m | 150 m / 30 m | 100 m / 30 m |
| Geofence warning margin (`fenceWarningMargin`, added to the pilot reaction time, see [Vehicle Fence Breaches](#vehicle-fence-breaches)) | 3 s | 3 s | 3 s | 3 s |
| GNSS quality for [geo-awareness](#geo-awareness): satellites / HDOP / accuracy (`minSatellites`, `maxHdop`, `maxHorizontalAccuracy`) | 6 / 5 / 5 m | 6 / 5 / 5 m | 6 / 5 / 5 m | 6 / 5 / 5 m |

For example a multirotor at 15 m/s without reported accuracy is warned 253 m or 20 s ahead, and when hovering 100 m away or 45 m below or above a zone. The geozone indicator page shows the current thresholds of the active vehicle, like "Warning 253 m / 20 s ahead at 15 m/s, 45 m above or below (Multirotor, 5 s reaction)", and the [alert log](#alert-log) records them with each alert.

The pilot can't lower them: **Extra warning time** and **Extra approach distance** in the settings only add to them. Custom builds replace the profiles with their own file `GeoZoneAlertProfiles.json` in the resource `:/Custom/GeoZone/GeoZoneAlertProfiles.json` (in `custom.qrc`: `<qresource prefix="/Custom/GeoZone"><file alias="GeoZoneAlertProfiles.json">res/GeoZoneAlertProfiles.json</file></qresource>`), in the format of the default file `src/GeoZone/Core/Evaluation/GeoZoneAlertProfiles.json`: `profiles` by class (`default`, `multirotor`, `fixedwing`, `vtol`), whose missing values come from the `default` class. A file with invalid values is ignored in favour of the default profiles.

### Guided Command Checks {#guided-command-checks}

Guided commands of the Fly View are checked too: the confirmation of **Go To Location**, **Change Loiter Radius**, **Orbit**, **Change Altitude**, **Pause**, **Takeoff**, **Return**, **Land**, setting the current mission item and starting, continuing or resuming the mission names the active restricted zones and U-space airspaces the vehicle would enter, including zones it is in which it would leave and enter again, updated as the altitude slider moves, and a go to location which enters zones is always confirmed. **Pause** is checked with the altitude of its slider like **Change Altitude**, since the vehicle holds at that altitude. For a vehicle in forward flight, **Go To Location** and **Change Loiter Radius** are checked with the loiter circle around the location, following its radius. **Arm**, **Takeoff** and **Start Mission** also warn when the vehicle is [on the ground inside a restricted zone](#on-the-ground). Starting the mission also checks the mission on the vehicle for a flight starting now and whether the vehicle's GeoFence excludes its prohibited zones.

- **Return** follows the return the vehicle flies from where it is, with its return parameters, to home, its rally points or the landing of the mission on it, or along the mission, like the [return paths](#return-and-contingency-paths) of the mission check, for example "The return to rally point 2 would enter geozones: ...". With **Smart RTL** checked the vehicle retraces the path it flew, which isn't checked.
- **Land** follows the descent to the ground below the vehicle (to the home altitude where the terrain isn't known), so zones from the ground up to a height below the vehicle count.
- Setting the current mission item follows the flight from the vehicle to the item and on along the mission, and resuming the mission the flight from the vehicle climbing to the altitude of the item it resumes at. An item without a coordinate counts from the item with a coordinate before it.

### Spoken Alerts, Repeats and Muting {#spoken-alerts}

Alerts are shown in a popup below the toolbar, the highest [level](#alert-levels) and most restrictive zone first, and spoken unless **Speak flight alerts** is off, each level starting with its word ("Warning: Entered geozone ..."). The popup closes by itself after 15 seconds, except while it asks for a [permission](#permission-question), and **Close** closes it. Alerts raised together, also by several vehicles, are spoken as one message naming the two most restrictive and counting the others ("..., and 2 more"). Spoken messages are at least 5 seconds apart, unless a more urgent alert comes: a higher level, a more restrictive zone, or entering rather than approaching it. While a vehicle stays inside a prohibited zone, "Still inside geozone ..." is shown again, and spoken, every 30 seconds until you confirm the permission to fly there or the vehicle leaves the zone; the reminders are shown also when the alerts aren't spoken. **Dismiss** in the popup acknowledges an alert which asks nothing, as do **Show** and **Acknowledge All** in the indicator. **Silence Speech for 1 min** in the popup (in its **•••** menu on phones and tablets, shown while the alerts are spoken) silences the speech for a minute (the indicator shows it, with **Resume Speech**): the alerts and their reminders are still shown, never hidden, but not spoken, except that entering a prohibited zone is still announced once, and alerts raised meanwhile are not spoken later.

### Permission to Fly in a Zone {#permission-question}

When a vehicle approaches or enters a prohibited, authorisation required, conditional or unknown zone, its alert asks **Do you have legal permission to fly here?**. **I do** answers it: the alerts about that zone are acknowledged and no longer shown, spoken or repeated for that vehicle (they are still logged) until it leaves the zone and its approach range, and the indicator marks the zone **Approved**. Coming back afterwards asks again. The answer is for one flight, like a [permit](#permits): when the vehicle disarms after having been armed, or disconnects, it ends, so a vehicle which landed inside the zone is warned on the ground again and alerted and asked when it arms for its next flight. For zones requiring an authorisation or a notification, **I do** acknowledges the [permit to enter](#permits) the zone for the rest of the flight instead, so coming back doesn't ask again. The popup stays open while a question is unanswered; once the vehicle has left the zone and its approach range the question no longer stands and the alert can be dismissed. Other alerts (U-space airspace, traffic, lost positions, vehicle fence breaches) only have **Dismiss**.

### Permits to Enter {#permits}

For zones requiring an authorisation (`REQ_AUTHORISATION`) or a [notification](#notifications) (unless prohibited), you acknowledge your permission to enter them per flight:

- before the flight, with **I Have Permission to Enter** in the zone details (click the zone on the map), or with **I Have Permission** next to the zone in the [pre-flight checklist](#pre-flight-checklist), which lists the zones of this kind the vehicle is in or the mission enters;
- in flight, by answering **I do** to the [permission question](#permission-question) of an alert about such a zone.

With a permit, approaching the zone is only logged and entering it shows the advisory "Entered geozone ... — permit acknowledged", which isn't spoken; the vehicle on the ground inside it may take off (the **Take-off position** item passes). Prohibited zones keep the approval until the vehicle leaves the zone or the flight ends. The geozone indicator page lists the **Permits This Flight** with the time and where they were acknowledged, and **Withdraw** ends one, so the zone alerts again. A permit doesn't replace an [authorisation](#authorisations), which is kept and checked during planning; it records that you have the permission for this flight. Permits end after the flight: when the vehicle disarms after having been armed, or when it disconnects; permits acknowledged without a vehicle connected end with the next flight. Each permit is logged in the [alert log](#alert-log) when acknowledged (event `permit`, the detail telling where: `zone details`, `pre-flight checklist` or `alert popup`) and when it ends (`permit_ended`, with `flight ended`, `vehicle disconnected` or `withdrawn`), with the time (UTC), the vehicle and the zone; alerts about the zone with a permit have the detail `permit`.

### Alert Actions {#alert-actions}

When the popup shows alerts about the active vehicle while it flies, its **•••** menu offers:

- **Hold** and **Return**: pause the vehicle or return it to launch.
- **Climb Above Ceiling**, when the zones of the alerts have a known ceiling above the vehicle: change the altitude to 30 m above the highest ceiling, offered when that is within the maximum altitude of the guided altitude slider (_Fly View_ settings).

Each opens the usual guided action confirmation of the Fly View, switching to it if needed, so the action is only sent once you confirm it with the slider; the climb sets the slider to the altitude above the ceiling, which you can still adjust.

### Altitude Strip and Approach Path {#altitude-strip}

While zones are above or below the active vehicle, the **Zone altitudes** strip on the left of the Fly View shows their floors and ceilings with the vehicle's altitude, above the ground when the terrain is known. Zones with the same restriction and limits, like the same zone from two sources, are drawn as one bar named after the first with the count of the others ("CTR Zurich (+1)"), and the names and limits are listed beside the bar. Where bars overlap, the most severe restriction is drawn on top (prohibited, then authorisation required, conditional, U-space, no restriction), and bars wholly above or below the altitudes shown are left out. While no floor or ceiling is within 300 m of the vehicle, for example on the ground below a high zone, the strip shrinks to a chip naming the most severe zone the vehicle is in; click it to expand the strip, and click the strip's title to shrink it again. It is dimmed and marked **Stale** while the vehicle's position is lost, and marked **AGL estimated** (with ≈ next to the height) when the height above ground comes from the terrain data of _QGroundControl_ rather than the vehicle.

When the active vehicle would enter a zone within the warning time of its [alert thresholds](#alert-thresholds) at its current velocity, the Fly View map draws its path to the entry point in the color of its [level](#alert-levels) (amber, red once the entry is imminent), with the zone name and the seconds left.

### Traffic in Geozones {#traffic}

With **Check traffic against geozones** on, the ADS-B and Remote ID aircraft received by _QGroundControl_ (see _Application Settings > ADSB Server_ and vehicles forwarding `ADSB_VEHICLE`) within 10 km of the active vehicle are checked against the active zones with the vehicles every second, using the altitude they report. The expanded geozone indicator lists the aircraft in or near zones in a **Traffic in or near Geozones** section, with their callsign (or ICAO address), the most restrictive zone, whether they are inside or how far from it, and their altitude. Without terrain data below an aircraft, the ground below the active vehicle is used for limits above the ground. The closest 100 aircraft are checked.

When an aircraft enters a zone, a low priority alert "Traffic ... entered geozone ..." is shown after the alerts of the vehicles and logged, but not spoken.

### Vehicle Fence Breaches {#vehicle-fence-breaches}

When a vehicle reports a breach of its own fence (MAVLink `FENCE_STATUS`), _QGroundControl_ shows it in the alert popup, the geozone indicator turns red and its page names the kind of breach (boundary, minimum or maximum altitude) and how many breaches the vehicle counted, until the vehicle reports the breach cleared. Below it, the popup and the indicator page name the fence action the autopilot takes and how to [override it](#override-fence-action); the popup stays open while the breach lasts. The breach is logged in the [alert log](#alert-log) (`fence_breached`). It isn't spoken by the geozone alerts: _QGroundControl_ already announces "boundary fence breached" and the flight mode the autopilot switches to, and ArduPilot sends its own "fence breached" message.

PX4 doesn't send `FENCE_STATUS`. For a vehicle which doesn't report its fence status, or stopped reporting it for 10 seconds (ArduPilot does while its fence is disabled), _QGroundControl_ takes the breach from the position the vehicle reports: the breach starts when the position is outside the [fence _QGroundControl_ holds for the vehicle](#geofence-warning) (inside an exclusion fence, outside the inclusion fences, beyond the distance from home or the altitude limits) and ends once the position has been back inside for 2 seconds. It is shown and logged once like a reported breach, without a breach count, and the override stays offered while the vehicle holds, returns or lands outside its fence. The margins of the fence don't count for it, and it is only as good as the fence _QGroundControl_ read from the vehicle.

#### PX4 and ArduPilot Fences {#px4-and-ardupilot-fences}

| | PX4 | ArduPilot (Copter 4.x) |
|---|---|---|
| Fence storage | 64 items, a firmware limit | Storage of the flight controller (estimated, 560 bytes for Copter on 16 KB boards) or the SD card (`BRD_SD_FENCE`), at most 255 vertices per polygon |
| Fence types | Inclusion and exclusion polygons and circles (inclusions as their union), `GF_MAX_HOR_DIST` from home, `GF_MAX_VER_DIST` above home | `FENCE_TYPE`: inclusion and exclusion polygons and circles (inclusions as their intersection, or union with `FENCE_OPTIONS` bit 1), the `FENCE_RADIUS` circle around home, `FENCE_ALT_MAX`, `FENCE_ALT_MIN` |
| Enabled | Unless `GF_ACTION` is None | `FENCE_ENABLE`, `FENCE_AUTOENABLE`, an RC switch or `MAV_CMD_DO_FENCE_ENABLE` |
| Action at a breach | `GF_ACTION`: none, warning, hold, return, flight termination, land | `FENCE_ACTION`: report only, RTL or land, land, SmartRTL or RTL or land, brake or land, SmartRTL or land; land beyond 100 m outside; disarm when landed |
| Margin | None | `FENCE_MARGIN` / `FENCE_MARGIN_XY`, counted by the geofence warning |
| Override | Flight mode change (Hold: mode switch only); termination can't be overridden | Flight mode change (10 s manual recovery), unless `FENCE_OPTIONS` bit 0; disabling the fence |
| In _QGroundControl_ | Fence import capacity 64 points; geofence warning; breach alert; **Take Control** (_Position_) | Fence import capacity estimated or from the SD card; geofence warning with the margin; breach alert; **Take Control** (_Loiter_), **Disable Fence** |

In both cases a flight towards a fenced zone gives, in this order: the geozone approach alert (spoken, asking for the permission for restricted zones), the geofence warning about 8 seconds before the fence (with the default profile) (red, above the geozone alerts, spoken), then the breach alert when the vehicle reports it (ArduPilot) or its position is past the fence (PX4), which replaces the warning. The vehicle stops at the fence, before the zone, so there is no entry alert. The breach is announced by _QGroundControl_ and the vehicle ("boundary fence breached", the new flight mode), not repeated by the geozone alerts.

#### Geofence Warning {#geofence-warning}

Before the vehicle's own fence makes it act, a dedicated **Geofence** warning tells the pilot what will happen (EN 4709-003 §6.2.2). While the vehicle's fence is enabled, each position the vehicle reports is checked with its velocity, horizontally and vertically, against the fence it stores (its inclusion and exclusion polygons and circles, read back after each upload or plan transfer), the circle around home and the altitude limits of its parameters:

- **ArduPilot**: the fences of `FENCE_TYPE` (polygons and circles, the `FENCE_RADIUS` circle around home, `FENCE_ALT_MAX` and `FENCE_ALT_MIN` above home), counting `FENCE_MARGIN` (or `FENCE_MARGIN_XY` horizontally) as part of the fence, and inclusion fences as their intersection, or their union with `FENCE_OPTIONS` bit 1. The fence counts as enabled with `FENCE_ENABLE`, with `FENCE_AUTOENABLE` once armed, or when the vehicle reports its fence enabled (the geofence flag of `SYS_STATUS`), like a fence enabled with an RC switch, which doesn't change `FENCE_ENABLE`. ArduPilot doesn't report a fence with `FENCE_ACTION` 0 (report only) as enabled.
- **PX4**: the polygons and circles, inclusion fences as their union, `GF_MAX_HOR_DIST` from home and `GF_MAX_VER_DIST` above home, unless `GF_ACTION` is None. With `GF_PREDICT` (on firmware versions which have it) PX4 acts when its trajectory would breach the fence, before the vehicle reaches it: the warning then reads "within 6 s" rather than "in 6 s", and the popup tells that the vehicle acts earlier than the time shown.

When the vehicle would reach the fence within the pilot reaction time of its [alert profile](#alert-thresholds) plus its `fenceWarningMargin` (8 seconds by default: a reaction time of 5 seconds, EN 4709-003 Annex A, plus 3 seconds to act; a profile with both at 0 gets these 8 seconds, since it would end the warning), the alert popup shows the warning above the geozone alerts, like "Geofence: RTL or land in 6 s, 42 m ahead", with:

- the **manoeuvre** the autopilot then takes, from `FENCE_ACTION` (ArduPilot Copter: report only, RTL or land, land, SmartRTL or RTL or land, brake or land, SmartRTL or land; Plane, also for VTOLs while they hover, and Rover have their own actions) or `GF_ACTION` (PX4: none, warning, hold, return, flight termination, land);
- the estimated **time** to the fence and the **distance** to it along the direction of travel, counting down;
- the estimated **intersection point**, as coordinates and altitude above home in the popup, and as a red mark with a red line from the vehicle on the Fly View map;
- how to [override](#override-fence-action) the manoeuvre.

When the vehicle rejects a fence upload, or a download or removal of the fence fails, _QGroundControl_ no longer has the vehicle's fence, while the vehicle usually keeps the one it had. The warning then keeps checking against the fence it knew, and reads the fence back from the vehicle once no plan transfer is in progress (up to 3 times, until a transfer succeeds).

The warning is spoken once and logged in the [alert log](#alert-log) (`fence_warning`, with the boundary, the distance, the time to the fence and the fence action). It ends when the vehicle no longer heads for the fence within 10 seconds, or breaches it. The prediction assumes a straight path at the current velocity: turns, braking and wind change it.

#### Overriding the Fence Action {#override-fence-action}

The pilot can always take over from the fence action (EN 4709-003 §6.2.1). While the warning or a breach is shown, the alert popup and the geozone indicator page offer:

- **Take Control (_mode_)** switches the flight mode to the one the pilot flies in: _Loiter_ (or _Position Hold_, _Altitude Hold_) for ArduPilot copters, _FBW A_ for planes, _QLoiter_ (or _QHover_) for ArduPilot VTOLs while they hover, _Manual_ for rovers, _Position_ for PX4. Changing the flight mode with the RC mode switch or the flight mode menu does the same. The popup reports whether the vehicle switched, and that the fence action is overridden only when the vehicle was breaching its fence: before the breach the vehicle still takes its fence action at the fence. The button is disabled while the vehicle wouldn't switch: during a breach with ArduPilot's `FENCE_OPTIONS` bit 0, and once PX4 terminates the flight (`GF_ACTION` 4).
- **Disable Fence** (ArduPilot only, after a confirmation) sends `MAV_CMD_DO_FENCE_ENABLE` to disable the fence until **Enable Fence** or a reboot. An RC switch with the _Fence_ option (`RCx_OPTION` 11) does the same. Once the vehicle accepted it, the geofence warning and the breach taken from the position stop, and the popup and the indicator page show _GEOFENCE disabled by the pilot_ with **Enable Fence**, until the vehicle accepts **Enable Fence**, reports its fence enabled again after reporting it disabled (like after its RC switch), or is armed with `FENCE_AUTOENABLE`. _QGroundControl_ reads `FENCE_ENABLE` again after both commands, for firmware versions which keep the state in it.

How the autopilots treat the override:

- **ArduPilot**: a flight mode change during a breach starts a _manual recovery_: the vehicle ignores the fence for 10 seconds, then acts only on a new breach (leaving the fence and breaching it again) or, for the altitude and circle fences, 20 m (100 m for Plane's circle) beyond where it breached. More than 100 m outside the fence, Copter lands. With `FENCE_OPTIONS` bit 0 (the default of Plane) the vehicle refuses mode changes until the breach clears, so disable the fence to take control.
- **PX4**: switching the flight mode ends the Hold, Return or Land action of `GF_ACTION`; the vehicle acts again only after it leaves the breach and breaches the fence again. Moving the sticks overrides Return and Land only when `COM_RC_OVERRIDE`/`COM_FAIL_ACT_T` allow it, and never Hold. Flight termination can't be overridden. PX4 doesn't accept `MAV_CMD_DO_FENCE_ENABLE`, so **Disable Fence** isn't offered.

_QGroundControl_ never commands a fence manoeuvre itself: Hold, Return and Climb in the alert popup are the pilot's actions, each confirmed.

### Conformance Monitoring {#conformance}

While the planned flight has a [planned start](#flight-time), _QGroundControl_ builds the [operational intent](#operational-intent) of the mission on the active vehicle, with the volumes and buffers of the export, and checks the flying vehicle against the volumes of the current time every second, like a U-space service provider monitors conformance. When the vehicle leaves them sideways, above or below, or flies when no volume applies (before the planned start or after the planned end, with the 15 minute buffer), the alert "Outside planned volume" says how far, like "Outside planned volume: 40 m above it". It is shown, spoken and logged like the zone alerts (event `outside_volume` in the [alert log](#alert-log), with the zone identifier `horizontal`, `above`, `below` or `time`), once until the vehicle is back inside. The geozone indicator page shows whether the vehicle is inside the planned volume. Without a planned start, or a mission whose altitudes are known, the monitoring is off.

### Alert Log {#alert-log}

Alerts are also appended to a `GeoZoneAlerts-<date>.csv` file per day (UTC) in the _Telemetry_ folder of the application load/save path, with the time, vehicle, position, altitude, terrain elevation, event (`approaching`, `entered`, `activated`, `activation_soon`, `position_lost`, `traffic_entered`, `outside_volume`, `on_ground`, `geo_awareness`, or `fence_warning` and `fence_breached` with the zone identifier `FENCE`, see [Vehicle Fence Breaches](#vehicle-fence-breaches)), distance, zone, predicted time to entry, the aircraft of traffic alerts, the alert id and, for the alerts of vehicles, the [alert thresholds](#alert-thresholds) which raised them (`warning_distance_m`, `warning_vertical_m`, `warning_time_s`) with the vehicle's `ground_speed_mps` and `vertical_speed_mps`, and the alert `level` (`warning`, `caution` or `advisory`, also on the pilot's answers), for reviewing flights. The log also records what the pilot was shown and answered, each with the id of its alert and the vehicle position and distance to the zone at that time: `notified` when the popup showed the alert (the `detail` column has the question when it asked for the [permission](#permission-question)), `approved` when the pilot answered **I do**, `dismissed` for **Dismiss** or **Close**, `muted` for **Silence Speech**, and `permit` and `permit_ended` for the [permits to enter](#permits). So a flight towards a restricted zone reads `approaching`, `notified`, `approved`, and the alerts while the zone is approved follow without `notified`. Alerts are written to the file within 2 seconds. The files are kept for the time set in **Keep alert logs and flight records** (forever by default): when a new file is started, the older ones are deleted.
The indicator lists the **Recent Alerts** of the session, newest first, followed by those logged earlier the same day. Alerts not looked at yet are shown in bold and counted in a red badge on the indicator; **Acknowledge All** clears them. **Show** opens the details of the alert's zone and marks the vehicle position at the time of the alert on the map for two minutes (click the mark to remove it).

### Flight Records {#flight-records}

When a vehicle disarms, _QGroundControl_ writes a record of its flight from arming to disarming to `GeoZones/GeoZoneFlight-<start>-vehicle<id>.json` in the _Telemetry_ folder, for the compliance records of the operation. It has the structure of the [JSON briefing](#pre-flight-briefing), with `schema` `qgc-geozone-flight-record`, evaluated for the track flown at the times it was flown:

- `flight` instead of `mission`: `vehicleId`, `vehicle`, `firmware`, `start`, `end`, `plannedStart` (of the [planned flight](#flight-time), null without), `durationSeconds`, `distanceMeters`, `maxAltitudeAMSL`, `takeoff` and `landing` (`lat`, `lon`, `altitudeAMSL`).
- `track`: the positions flown with their `time`, `lat`, `lon` and `altitudeAMSL`, one when the vehicle moved 10 m, climbed or descended 5 m or after 30 seconds, and every other one beyond 2000 positions.
- `zones`: the zones the track entered (`entered`), with the times and altitudes of each crossing, and the restricted zones it passed within 500 m (`nearby`), like the briefing.
- `permits`: the [authorisations](#authorisations) of the zones entered: `zoneKey`, `identifier`, `name`, `permit`, `validFrom`, `validTo` and `coversFlight`.
- `alerts`: the rows of the [alert log](#alert-log) of the vehicle during the flight, the alerts and your answers to them, as objects of the log columns (`time_utc`, `vehicle`, `latitude`, `longitude`, `altitude_amsl_m`, `event`, `zone_identifier`, ...). When the log can't be written or read, the alerts raised during the flight.
- `sources`: the versions and provenance of the geozone data covering the track.
- `operator` and `aircraft`, `null` unless [operator details](#operator-details) are included.

Records are kept like the alert logs, for the time set in **Keep alert logs and flight records**.

## Limitations {#limitations}

- The checks and alerts are advisory and depend on the data loaded: always check the official sources and obtain any required authorisation before flying.
- AIXM schedules are not read, so AIXM zones are always active, and holes of AIXM airspaces are not read. Parts of AIXM boundaries defined by other features, like borders and coasts, are replaced by straight lines, with a warning that the zone is approximate.
- European NOTAMs and FAA NOTAMs from the NOTAM Management Service (NMS) are not supported yet: _QGroundControl_ has no reader for their GeoJSON or AIXM events, so the NMS isn't offered by **Add Service...**. FAA TFRs are available without credentials from the **United States TFRs (FAA)** feed. A TFR whose NOTAM details can't be downloaded is treated as prohibited at all times and altitudes; check the NOTAM for exceptions.
- Flight levels are converted with the QNH setting and a 4 % margin, and heights above the WGS84 ellipsoid with the EGM96 geoid, so these limits are approximate. Results relying on such limits, unknown terrain or altitudes, coarse terrain or schedules which can't be fully read are marked as [approximate](#approximate-results).
- Schedules with unknown days are taken as every day, which isn't marked as approximate; the zone details show the problem.
- Return and contingency paths are estimates, see [Return and Contingency Paths](#return-and-contingency-paths).
- The [operational intent](#operational-intent) is only exported: it isn't submitted to a U-space or UTM service provider, checked against other flights or approved, and only includes the operator and aircraft details of the Remote ID settings.
- GeoFences made from zones have no altitude limits, no holes and no schedule (zones active only part of the flight aren't fenced), and the ArduPilot fence storage is estimated.
- Secrets of services are stored unencrypted, in a file only your user account can read. Feeds are only checked by SHA-256, not signatures; offline packs are signed only in builds with OpenSSL, by default desktop Linux builds.

Developers can find the architecture of the geozone module in the [Geozones developer guide](../../qgc-dev-guide/geozones.md).
